# Build-system contract before engine design

Status: design constraints and hand-computed acceptance cases. Ordinary item, distribution,
cost and input modules are accepted; policy/CLI integration and frontier stretch are tracked
in `docs/STATE.md`. Authority: `docs/BRIEF.md` section 3b and owner instruction,
read 2026-09-20; decision D7. All numbers below are invented mathematical fixtures, not PoE facts,
market prices, or PoB measurements.

## Required behavior

- A stage returns an outcome distribution: probability for each resulting mod set, or for each
  explicitly defined scored class. Keep failure/unsupported mass visible. Monte Carlo reports
  sample count and seed alongside estimated probabilities; it does not claim exact probabilities.
- The item spec accepts required mods OR a value table keyed by mod group and tier plus a
  stopping threshold. The helmet remains the required-mod demo.
  `examples/bow_value_table.yaml` uses clearly marked invented values. The accepted input spec
  defines JSON-compatible YAML and assigns zero utility to missing table entries.
- Success is a predicate over the distribution: required mods satisfied, or utility >= threshold.
  A policy includes a stopping rule and action/continue/restart/failure decision points.
- A policy result carries expected utility, expected cost, cost p90, irreversible-failure risk,
  required rare inputs, steps/decisions, and exact versus estimated method. Declare whether
  utility is conditional on success and how budget exhaustion or failure is scored.
- Return a Pareto frontier for the evaluated policies. A policy with greater utility and lower
  cost must remain distinguishable from a single best exact-target recipe. Risk and loadout
  constraints remain visible; do not silently treat missing risk as zero.

Retain concrete state detail needed by later stages. Equal-score items can have different open
slots, groups, fractures or influence, so equality of utility alone does not justify transition
aggregation. Exact PoB valuation of a concrete item is a later integration owned by the lead.

## Synthetic acceptance case: one stage, two stopping rules

Invented stage outcomes: A has probability 1/2 and utility 0; B has probability 1/3 and utility 6;
C has probability 1/6 and utility 10. Total probability is 1 and stage expected utility is 11/3.
These are probabilities supplied to the test, not generated by a game action.

For independent identical trials, price 2 per trial, no setup cost, unlimited retries, and no
irreversible failure:

| stopping rule | success probability | expected attempts | expected total cost | attempts p90 | cost p90 | terminal expected utility |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| utility >= 6 | 1/2 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 8 | 22/3 |
| utility >= 10 | 1/6 | 6 | 12 | 13 | 26 | 10 |

Derivation: each policy has geometric attempt count with CDF `1-(1-p)^n`; p90 is the smallest
integer n >= 1 for which this is >= 0.9. At p=1/2, F(3)=7/8 < 0.9 and F(4)=15/16 >= 0.9.
At p=1/6, F(12)=0.887843... < 0.9 and F(13)=0.906536... >= 0.9. Terminal distribution for
threshold 6 is B:2/3, C:1/3, giving utility 22/3. Both policies are nondominated on expected
utility versus expected cost. The stage distribution itself does not change when the threshold
changes. Reset costs, dependence, budgets and irreversible failures invalidate this simple
geometric fixture unless explicitly modeled; do not extend the formula silently.

## Gates before implementation

The worker spec for any calculator must include distribution normalization and more than one
threshold over the same distribution, with a hand-computed known answer. A Monte Carlo spec
must compare outcome frequencies as well as aggregate success. A ranker spec must verify
p90 and terminal utility separately from stage utility. No game probability is accepted by
passing these synthetic cases.
