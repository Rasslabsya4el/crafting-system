# Vendor recipes are start-item sources chosen outside the solver

Five vendor recipes make an item with a chosen mod from cheap goods. The owner put all five into v1 ([#102](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/102), decision comment of 2026-11-01); the rules are the research note [`2026-10-31-vendor-recipes.md`](../research/2026-10-31-vendor-recipes.md) (PR #327). This is build ticket 1 of 3 ([#329](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/329)): the decisions, the claims and the step tables. The start options built from them are [#330](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/330); the report and a benchmark are [#331](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/331).

## The five recipes

| Recipe | Input | Other goods | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boots movement speed (`boots_movement_speed`) | Normal boots | a Quicksilver Flask and an Orb of Augmentation per step, 5 steps | Magic boots, only movement speed, 10 / 15 / 20 / 25 / 30% (T6 up to T2) |
| Weapon flat damage (`weapon_physical`, `weapon_fire`, `weapon_cold`, `weapon_lightning`) | a Normal weapon | a Granite (Physical), Ruby (Fire), Sapphire (Cold) or Topaz (Lightning) Flask and an Orb of Augmentation per step | Magic weapon, only that added damage, up to T2 (Physical) or T3 / T2 (elemental, below) |
| Jewellery chaos resistance (`jewellery_chaos_resistance`) | a Normal ring or amulet | an Amethyst Flask and an Orb of Augmentation per step, 5 steps | Magic jewellery, only chaos resistance, 5-10% up to 26-30% (T6 up to T2) |
| Spell-gem weapon (`spell_gem_<tag>`, five tags) | a Normal wand, sceptre, rune dagger or staff | two or more quality gems of the tag, 40% quality in all; no flask, no orb | Magic weapon with +1 to Level of all Spell Skill Gems of the tag (+2 on a staff) |
| Minion helmet (`minion_helmet`) | a Normal helmet | two or more quality gems with the Minion tag, 40% in all | Magic helmet with +1 to Level of all Minion Skill Gems |

A tier-up recipe is a chain: from a Normal base each step gives the next rung of the mod's ladder, taking the Magic item the step before made, so 30% movement speed is five recipes in a row. The other recipes of the wiki page (the two Whetstone recipes, the Onyx amulet, the 5-to-1 and the currency recipes) are out of v1: they give low tiers, random items or currency, and their claims stay in the research folder. Jewels have no recipe that picks a mod.

## A recipe is a start-item source, chosen outside the solver

A vendor recipe is **not an action of a route**. The recipe wipes every other mod of the input, so inside a route it is worth using only on a Normal or near-empty item, which is a start item. The planner offers the plain start item and each recipe-made start item, **plans each of them, and keeps the cheapest total** (the start item's price, which is the recipe's goods, plus the plan's mean cost). The choice is made outside the solver: one plan per start option. It works with today's solver and with the solver that takes donors (#312); nothing in `solver` learns about recipes. The comparison and the report are #330 and #331.

This **amends ADR-0043's "acquisition methods are not modelled" for vendor recipes only**. A Genesis-born item is just a pasted start item whose price is whatever the user pastes; a vendor recipe is priced from market inputs and so changes the answer, which is why it is modelled. A recipe-made item is a priced start option, not a second way to paste one.

## What the recipe data holds

`crafting_system.items.vendor_recipes` lists, for each recipe, the base kinds it takes and the step table: at each step the flask, the number of Orbs of Augmentation, the gems (tag, count, total quality), and the mod and tier the step gives, counted from the top of the mod's ladder (T1 is the best). It reads the claims of [`vendor.yaml`](../../src/crafting_system/rules/claims/vendor.yaml) and holds no number of its own; every claim in the file is cited by some recipe, and a test says so. A recipe asked for a base kind it does not take is refused with `BaseKindError` (a staff takes the physical recipe of its own two-hand ladder; a sceptre takes no chaos spell-gem recipe, because the data has no such mod). The input must not be fractured or corrupted (`vendor.refused_on_fractured_and_corrupted_inputs`).

## The elemental cap is a rule axis

The wiki caps the Fire, Cold and Lightning chains at **T3** (eight recipes from Normal); Craft of Exile's table says **T2** (nine recipes) for these as for every tier-up recipe. The data holds a rung at each, and no other source decides (research O-1). So the cap is a **rule axis** `elemental_cap` of two readings (ADR-0033): `wiki` (the plan's, T3) and `coe` (T2). The answer shows the cost over both beside the plan's. The physical cap (T2) is agreed by both sources and has no axis. No in-game check is asked for now; if the conflict stays open it goes on the owner's list ([#182](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/182)). Axis machinery for the readings belongs to #330, which prices the start option at each reading.

## The base type and the item level are kept, marked `approximate`

The wiki says the recipes make "a new base item (with randomized base defences)" and does not say whether the base type stays; the recipe page and the Item level page also word the item level of the result differently. The plan keeps **the input's base type and the input's item level**. Both claims are `approximate` (ADR-0028), and so is the rule for which gems count: the recipe data takes uncorrupted skill gems only (`vendor.gem_qualification_is_uncorrupted_skill_gems`). Every answer that uses a vendor start item lists them. Defence rolls are not targets, so a new roll does not matter.

## Prices

A recipe's price is the sum of its goods: the Normal base (priced like any base, with the item level the target needs, because the result's level is the input's), the flasks, the Orbs of Augmentation and the gems. Orbs of Augmentation come from the price file's currency line. **Flasks and quality gems come from the trade API** (a Normal flask of the base type; the cheapest set of two or more gems of the tag whose quality adds up to 40%), **and a hand price overrides them** (the hand-price file, [#323](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/323)). Time and clicks are free, as in ADR-0007. The recipe is not random, so the cost of a chain is the sum of its steps and needs no chance node (ADR-0011): for boots, `base + 5 * (flask + augmentation)`.

## What this changes

- ADR-0043 and ADR-0008 each point here (an amendment line in each).
- `vendor.yaml` joins the rules registry with 14 claims, four of them `approximate` (the item level of the result, its base type, which gems count, the elemental cap). The claims the research drafted for the other recipes stay in the research folder.
- The step tables are data; the start options and their pricing (#330, the amendment below) and the route view and benchmark (#331) are built on them.

## Amendment: the start options and the choice ([#330](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/330), 2026-11-01)

Build ticket 2 of 3. The code is `cli/_start_options.py` (the list), `cli/_starts.py` (the choice), `cli/_start_report.py` (the words) and `cli/_start_types.py` (the records).

**The list of start options is a list of priced candidates, each with its source.** A candidate is a concrete item, its price, the goods that make the price and a `source`: `plain` (the target's own start item) or `vendor recipe`. It is not a recipe-only code path: research [#337](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/337) asks whether the planner should choose what to buy at any state, and a bought item is one more source whose candidates a trade query prices. A source is a function from the game data, the target and the prices to a `StartList` (candidates, refusals, rule axes); `plan_with_starts` takes the list of sources, and the choice and the report read only the candidate's fields, never its source. A test adds a "bought" source and it wins.

**Which recipe starts are options.** Only recipes that take the target's base kind (the item class in lower case). A step count is an option when the Magic item that many steps make meets some requirement of the target on its own (`Requirement.is_met_by`: the mod, the tier limit and the roll limit), so only step counts that reach a tier the target accepts are listed. Among step counts that meet the same requirements only the cheapest is planned: a later step costs more and leaves the target the same item, so it is listed as refused with the reason `dominated`. Other reasons a recipe is refused, each in the plan's explanation (text and JSON): `no target mod` (no requirement counts a mod of the ladder the recipe climbs; the ladder is the mods of one mod type, because the "+N to Level of all X Skill Gems" mods of every tag share one family), `cap does not reach the asked tier` (the recipe's last step is below what the target asks), `item level below the mod's required level` (the result keeps the input's item level, claim `approximate`, and a mod needs its level; this is the same limit the mod pool applies, not a new claim), `roll not guaranteed`, `no price` and `mod cannot spawn on the base`.

**Rolls.** A recipe rolls the mod inside the rung's range and a start item is one concrete item. A step is an option when it meets the requirement at the lowest roll of its range (so at every roll), and the item is made at that roll. A rung whose range straddles the target's roll limit is refused with `roll not guaranteed`; a chance start item (a distribution over start states) is out of this ticket.

**The choice.** The plain start item is planned first, then the recipe starts from the cheapest to the dearest; each by `make_plan` on the target with that candidate as its start item and its price as the start price (`start_price_of`), so `restart` rebuys the recipe item. The answer is the candidate with the smallest **total**, the start price plus the plan's expected cost still to pay; a tie goes to the earlier one. A candidate whose start price alone is not below the best total found is not planned (the cost still to pay is never negative), and the report says so; this shortcut changes no answer. Nothing in `solver` learns what a candidate is.

**The rule axis.** The elemental cap is read at both readings. A step count past the wiki cap exists only at the `coe` reading, and the candidate and the refusals say at which readings they hold. The plan is the `wiki` reading (the first value of the axis); the cheapest start is also found at the `coe` reading, and the answer lists the cheapest total at each reading and their range (the way the readings of ADR-0033 are shown); a candidate planned once is not planned again for another reading. When the axis changes the cheapest total, the claim `vendor.weapon_elemental_damage_tier_up` is listed among the approximate inputs.

**Prices.** The base is `base_price_name(base, item level, influences)` (a Normal base), the flasks are "Quicksilver Flask", "Granite Flask", "Ruby Flask", "Sapphire Flask", "Topaz Flask" and "Amethyst Flask", the orb is "Orb of Augmentation", and the gems are one line per tag, "Vendor recipe gems (fire, 40% quality)": the cheapest set of two or more uncorrupted skill gems of the tag whose quality adds up to 40%. All come from the price file with the hand prices on top (`load_prices`). A recipe with a missing line is refused and the names that are missing are listed. A flask line can be fetched with the existing buy-spec fetcher (its `name` is the line); the gem lines have no query yet and are hand prices until one exists.

**The setting.** `recipe_starts` under `[planner]` in `crafting.toml` (default on) or `--no-recipe-starts` turns recipe starts off; the plan is then `make_plan`'s, with no `Plan.starts`, byte for byte. With the setting on and no recipe taking the target's base kind, nothing changes and nothing is printed; with a base a recipe takes, the numbers stay the same when the plain start wins, and the report adds the block of options and refusals. `--route` plans from the plain start only, because a given route is written for it. With a `--budget`, each start option is planned within the whole budget. [#323](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/323)'s "turn a method off" will generalise it. `--start-price` is the price of the plain start item only. Whenever a recipe start is in the answer, the approximate claims of its recipe are listed with the answer.

## Amendment: the first step in the report and the benchmark V01 ([#331](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/331), 2026-11-01)

Build ticket 3 of 3. The code is `cli/_start_step.py` (the step), `cli/_start_report.py` (its text and JSON), `cli/_route_view.py` (row 1 of the page) and `cli/_benchmark.py` (the route V01).

**A recipe start is the first step of the report.** When the chosen start is made at a vendor, the report opens with a block *First step, before S1* and does not call the item "bought". `first_step(candidate, data, target)` builds it from the chosen candidate and the recipe data alone and returns None for any other source, so a bought-item source of research #337 prints its own line and the choice never reads the step. It holds:

- **the bill**, over all recipes of the chain: the Normal base, the flasks, the Orbs of Augmentation and the gems, each with its count, its price and where the price came from (the candidate's `goods`, the numbers the start price is the sum of); the gems are one priced line, the cheapest set, and say what set it is ("2 or more uncorrupted skill gems with the Minion tag, 40% quality in all");
- **the recipes one by one**, each with what it takes and the mod and exact tier it gives (`+10% increased Movement Speed, T6` up to `T2`); a chain says that each recipe takes the Magic item the one before made and the first takes the Normal base;
- **the item that comes back**: the Magic base, its item level, and only the one mod with its tier, because a recipe wipes every other mod; a mod that rolls inside a range (flat damage) says that the recipe rolls it and the plan counts the lowest roll, which every roll meets;
- **the start price**, the sum of the bill, and that a restart makes the item again at this price.

The same step is `start_options.first_step` in the JSON (the bill with roles, the recipes with tiers, the item, the price; null when the start is not made at a vendor) and row 1 of the route page: titled *Vendor recipe: ...*, with the fields *Hand the vendor*, *Recipes* (*Recipe* for a single one) and *Cost per use*, the item card of what comes back, and a *Start* field on the goal card that sets the chosen start beside the others. The restart row says "make the start item at the vendor again". A buy spec (`--buy-spec`) describes a bought item, so a recipe start gets no trade link. The old line "hand the vendor: ..." inside the options block is gone: the bill is told once, in the step. A plain start, and a plan with recipe starts off, print none of this and read as before.

**The risk grid is measured for the kept start only.** `make_plan(with_risk=False)` solves and leaves `Plan.risk` None; the choice compares the means and `add_risk` measures the p50, p90 and CVaR of the one plan it keeps. The grid costs minutes where the solve costs seconds when the cheapest orb is a Transmutation (ticket #190), and the start that loses was measured for nothing. The answer is unchanged: the means come from the solver, and the kept plan's risk is the one `make_plan` would give.

**Benchmark V01.** `examples/targets/v01-minion-helmet.yaml`: a Lion Pelt of item level 84 with `+1 to Level of all Minion Skill Gems` and top-tier Life, from a Normal base with Transmutation, Alteration, Augmentation and the Regal Orb. The Minion mod is 250 of 114,675 weight of the pool (one draw in 459), so the plain start finds it rarely; the Minion helmet recipe makes a Magic helmet that holds exactly that mod for the base and the gems. At the committed prices (`tests/fixtures/prices/vendor-v01-2026-11-01.json`: poe.ninja plus hand prices for the base and for the gem line, which the refresher does not fetch yet, ticket #360) the recipe start costs 8 Chaos and 866.40 in all against 1,348.94 for the plain Normal helmet. `BenchmarkRoute` gains `start` (`ExpectedStart`: the key of the start the plan should choose and the explanation of why it wins) and a `route` that may be None: no guide writes a route for a recipe-made start item, so V01 has no given-route half; its entry records the expected and the chosen start, every option's total, the refusals and the first step, and `matches` says whether they agree. The entry is recorded, never a failing run. The goldens are `tests/golden/v01_minion_helmet.txt`, `.json` and `.html`. A boots example from the research note (30% movement speed plus Life, with the same four orbs, planned once at the same prices) was tried too and does not show the recipe: the plain start finds 30% movement speed cheaply by Alteration spam (0.1 Chaos a click), so the plain start won there, 531 Chaos in all against 1,017 from the five-recipe boots. The recipe pays where the mod it makes is rare in the pool and expensive to hit, as the Minion gems mod is.
