# Big graphs: packed arrays first, then bounds and an envelope search; cuts are classified by what they guarantee, and fixed rails are never defaults

**Draft** for [ticket #312](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/312) (research note `docs/research/2026-11-01-exact-merging-and-cuts.md` holds the numbers; the three outside reports are `2026-11-01-dr-1-…`, `-dr-2-…`, `-dr-3-…`). Not accepted: the owner decides which of the tickets below are cut. The number 0063 may need to change if another draft takes it first.

R04 (177,242 states, 535.9 M transitions), R14 (816,153 states), the strand target S02 (106,002 states, 76.6 billion transitions) and R08 with the Alchemy did not solve exactly. The question was whether their states differ in detail no decision sees, so that an exact merge would shrink them. Measured, it depends on the route: S02 shrinks 5,300x (20 classes), R13 and S13 51x and 313x, R05 and R10 100x and more, R08 with the Alchemy 17x, but R04 only 2.0x and R14 1.4x. The measurement shows how much merges, not why: routes that type their junk as numbered family types merge 5,300x (S02) and 1.4x (R14) alike. What stopped R04 and R14 is the memory of the solver for the transitions (about 32 B each, 17 GB for R04), a serial Python walk, and the absence of any cut by cost before or during the walk. Solved on packed arrays after the exact merge, R04 = 69,716.06 chaos, R14 = 5,786.62 and S02 = 33,879 (1-chaos prices) in minutes. The values move with main (a second snapshot gives R04 = 69,257.54 and R14 = 5,784.49: as far as the two quotients show, the chances of the Exalted Orb and the essences changed in between), so the goldens are taken when the packed solver lands. The outside reports' verdict (the SSP model is right, the unit of computation is too concrete, optimise while generating) is borne out in one respect: the optimal policy of R04 and R14 reaches about a tenth of the reachable states (note, "The envelope"). It is not borne out in another: exact lumping alone does not shrink R04 or R14 much.

## Decision

1. **The first lever is the representation: packed arrays and a packed policy iteration are the production path for large graphs.** Transitions as numpy arrays (destination, chance, cost per action, kernels shared), self-loops eliminated into exit costs, policy iteration from a proper start policy (the shortest-path tree of the luckiest path lowers the distance to the goal with positive chance, so it is proper), each evaluation an iterative linear solve, the Bellman residual reported and certified the way `solver/_certify.py` certifies a component. The exact merge (the coarsest partition with the same goal status, action costs and outcome chances over the classes) is folded in as a pre-step because it costs little: 25 s on R04, and it cuts R04's edges 3.1x, which is what lets the arrays fit; on S02 it is the only way. The object solver stays for small routes and for everything with inputs, variants or Locks. **Acceptance:** the packed solve is cross-checked against the object solver on R05, R08, R10, R12 and R13 to 1e-9 relative (the research script is already within 3e-12 on them) before R04's and R14's values become goldens (taken on the main of that day).
2. **The walk is packed and parallel.** States and kernels as arrays instead of objects, and the walk split over processes. Coordinate with #250 (PR #326): its pool and `Abstraction.prime` survive; its BFS prefetch hook does not. Measured now: R04 245–457 s and 1.3 GB, R14 1,721–1,962 s and 8 GB, S02 828–1,156 s and 2.7 GB (all in the research walker, which keeps one record per kernel key; the production walk of R04 took 3,526 s and 6.3 GB in PR #270). **Targets by machine class:** on a 4-vCPU runner (16 GB) R14 under 10 minutes and 4 GB; on a weak PC with 8 GB, R04 is estimated to fit (walk 1.3 GB plus an estimated 3 GB of packed arrays per kernel, no measurement behind it; the research script measured 6.0 GB) and R14 is not possible today (8 GB for the walk alone), with a packed walk estimated at 1 to 2 GB (not measured).
3. **Bounds, then an envelope search.** A research ticket first: a lower bound that sees the junk mod types (pattern databases and cost partitioning from report 1 are the starting point). The prize is measured: a perfect bound would need 13% of R04's classes and 11% of R14's, and the cheap bounds tried (luckiest path, optimistic coarse models over status, rarity, mods per side, fractured ids) give 0.5–4.1% of the cost on them and cut nothing. Only when a bound reaches half of the exact cost on R04 and R14 is an iLAO*-style envelope search built, with executable upper-bound policies (a given route or a cheap first solve), per-state action elimination, and a stop when the root decision is proved. *(Corrected by the update "Research #376" below: the bound needed the crafted mods, not the junk mod types, and a strong bound still makes a weak exact cut on R04.)*
4. **Cuts are classified by what they guarantee, and the report says which ones ran.** *Exact*: an action is not expanded when its cost with lower bounds exceeds the best action's cost with upper bounds (ADR-0031 raised from offers to states; nothing is lost). *Tolerance, only with a stated guarantee*: an outcome is lumped only when `p * (UB - LB)` is below the margin the bounds leave the choice; the report states `V_opt <= V* <= V_pess`, the relative gap and that every settled choice is unchanged (measured: every state settled on R05 and R10, none on R04). *Safety net*: a time or memory limit stops the run and says so, and the report lists the limits that were binding.
5. **Fixed rails and thresholds are not defaults.** A ceiling of 1,000 uses makes R11 (1,086 chaos) infinite; lumping outcomes below 1e-3 moves R13 from 6,300 to between 24 and 16,800 chaos and R08 with the Alchemy by +4.3%; 1e-4 and 1e-5 changed nothing on any route measured, but nothing says why. A user's own rails and inventories stay a user input (#323), and report 2's warning applies to them: with a hard budget in the state the stationary self-loop macro is no longer exact.
6. **An exact merge inside the walk is not built.** On R04 and R14 a canonical key would gain 2.0x and 1.4x at most. For S02, where it would save the 19-minute walk, none of the nine simple projections tried (plus the identity; on R08 and S02) is both exact and merging: dropping or coarsening the slots is not exact, keeping them merges nothing. A key would have to come from the partition itself (for instance from what `Reads` says the actions can read); that is a research ticket, not a build.
7. **The strand targets S02, S11 and S13 are in the tractability set** next to R04, R14 and R08 with the Alchemy (as today's loaders read them: strand words removed). S11 and S13 are 4,066 states and 13 classes; S02 is solved after the merge.

## What the measurements say about each part

- **Packed solve:** R04's merged graph (89,778 classes, 414,544 actions, 171 M edges) in 72 to 155 s; the research script's memory is 6.0 GB after loading and flattening and 14.4 GB at the end of the full cut analysis, so the production version must be leaner (per kernel, not per action). R14's (589,930 classes, 6.9 M edges) in 19 s; S02's (20 classes) under a second. The object solver died at 15.7 GB on R04.
- **Envelope:** the optimal policy reaches 11,767 of R04's 89,778 classes (at most 18,911 of 177,242 states, 10.7%) and 65,149 of R14's 589,930 (at most 87,271 of 816,153, 10.7%); with a 0.1% tolerance the envelope is already 78% of R04's states and 74% of R14's, with 1% all of R04's. Pairs: 11,766 of 414,544 on R04 and 65,149 of 1.92 M on R14; all offers of R04 and R14 appear in the envelope, so root pruning of offers removes none there.
- **Cuts:** the luckiest-path bound with 32 sweeps is 0.6% of R04's cost and cuts nothing; the coarse optimistic bounds give 97% on R13 but 1.4–3.5% on R04 and 0.5–4.1% on R14. Most actions not chosen are within 1% of the best (R04: 95%), so an exact cut cannot remove them, and a tolerance cut only with a settled margin.

## Consequences

- The proof sketch of the exact cut: `LB(s') <= V*(s') <= UB(s')` for every state; an optimistic model has an optimal value at most the exact one because a concrete policy is a policy of the model that picks the action of the member it is in; an action whose lower Q exceeds the best upper Q is in no optimal policy, so the optimal value of the cut graph is that of the whole.
- The packed solve is a second solver. Both must agree on the routes both can hold; a test keeps it so.
- A walk guided by bounds needs the bounds before the walk, so the bound must come from an abstraction that does not need the full graph (the variants level of ADR-0023 is the nearest existing piece).

## Proposed tickets (in this order)

1. **Packed solve with the exact merge** (`solver/`): the policy-iteration solve of `research/312/prune_analysis.py` and the partition refinement of `research/312/exact_classes.py`, per kernel, with certified residual; cross-checked to 1e-9 on R05, R08, R10, R12, R13; R04, R14 and S02 end to end through `make_plan`; goldens for R04 and R14. Done when R04, R14 and S02 give their exact value through the planner.
2. **Packed, parallel walk** (`abstraction/`, `cli/`): states and kernels as arrays, split over processes; coordinated with #250; targets per machine class above. Done when R14 is under 10 minutes and 4 GB on a 4-vCPU runner and the note says what an 8 GB PC does.
3. **A lower bound that sees the junk mod types** (research): pattern databases and cost partitioning, buildable without the full graph; measured on R04, R14 and S02 against the 13% / 11% perfect-bound prize. Done when a bound is at least half of the exact cost on R04 and R14, or a note says it cannot be. *(Done by #376: see the update below.)*
4. **Envelope search** (build, after 3): iLAO*-style expansion from the start with the bound, an executable upper bound, per-state action elimination and a stop when the root decision is proved; measured against the full walk on R05, R13, R04, R14.
5. **Report the cuts and the limits** (`cli/`): the report lists which cut kinds ran, the tolerance and what it guarantees, and the limits that were binding; no cut by a fixed count or chance. Done when a binding time or memory limit makes the plan say "not exact, stopped at …" and a test covers it. Fits #323's user rails.
6. **An exact key in the walk** (research, optional): from `Reads`/`Adds`, checked against the partition on S02, R13 and R05. Not to be built before a key equals the partition there.

## Not proposed

A rail or a chance threshold with a default; lumping by a fixed chance; a canonical key for R04 and R14.

## Update: ticket 1 built (#374)

Ticket 1 is cut and built; the other tickets are unchanged. What is in the product:

- **The packed solve** (`solver/_packed.py`, `_packed_merge.py`, `_packed_solve.py`, `_packed_model.py`): a graph as flat arrays with one stored kernel per distinct distribution; the exact merge by partition refinement (distributions and signatures compared as exact integer bytes, never hashes); proper states by shortest-path sweeps; policy iteration from the luckiest-path policy, each policy evaluated by a dense solve (up to 1,500 states) or BiCGSTAB; a certificate per state (`certify`). **ADR-0010 is amended**: `solver/` stays standard-library only except the files named `_packed*.py`, which use numpy (`tests/test_solver_independence.py` allows exactly that). The generic `solve_packed_model(model, starts)` returns the same `Solution` as `solve`.
- **The certificate runs on the unmerged graph**, with the class values copied to the members and the operator without the self-loop compiled out. It is therefore also an independent check of the merge and of the solve: a wrong partition cannot give wrong values and pass it (the set of states with no proper policy is taken from the quotient, which lumpability makes exact). Per state it uses the cheapest action cost `g(s)`, the cost `k(s)` of the greedy action, the residuals `e(s) = (TV - V)+`, `d(s) = (V - TV)+`, each widened by the float64 rounding of the sum, and gives `max g/(g+d) V <= V* <= min k/(k-e) V`. The bound may exceed the tolerance by the rounding of long sums (S02's kernels have 10^5 terms: 6.9e-8); a solve whose bound is above 1e-6 raises `ConvergenceError`.
- **The walk** (`cli/_packed.py`) shares one kernel per `Abstraction.kernel_key` (serial; ticket 2 packs and parallelises it). `make_plan(solver="auto")` walked packed first and left a graph of at most `EXACT_TRANSITIONS` (2 M, what the object solver has been run on) to the object solver, so small routes were unchanged (since #535 `auto` is the envelope search wherever its bound can be built, and this is `auto` only where it cannot: see the update of #535); `"packed"` always uses this route (with 40 offers or more on the twin-merged offers, without the bound pruning), `"object"` never. The bound pruning of 40 offers or more (`solve_pruned`) still solves by the object solver; routing its rounds is left open.
- **The policy** of a packed plan is a lazy mapping from the states the policy reaches to their `Action`s, built when asked for. **The risk** is evaluated on the merged chain (lumpability: same mean, odds and quantiles). A merged chain of more than 2 M transitions gets its exact odds, mean and variance and no quantile grid, and the plan's note says so: the budget DP of `risk/` outgrew a 16 GB runner on R04's 4.7 M-transition chain (stack dumps: `_budget._expand`). The moments of a chain that long are solved on packed arrays too (the object solver's sweeps do not converge in an hour on it).

Measured on GitHub runners (ubuntu, 4 vCPU, 16 GB), through `make_plan` on main of 2026-11-02 (the walk time varies 1.9x between runners; the whole `make_plan` took 461-650 s for R04, 1,187 s for R14, 842-851 s for S02):

## Update: R04 and R14 end to end (#70)

The CLI returns both policies. What was added on top of ticket 1, and what the reported numbers are:

- **No refusal for a graph the object solver cannot hold.** `make_plan(budget=...)` decides as before (ADR-0023). When no approximate level exists and the graph is beyond the object solver (R04 and R14 have an Attack/Caster metamod, so no level), it used to refuse; it now solves exactly on the packed solver and says so in `Plan.note` (`_plan._packed_note`). `solver="object"` still refuses, and so does a query of 40 offers or more (the bound pruning is object-only). `--progress` prints the phases and the peak memory to stderr.
- **The policy's chain is lumped exactly** (`solver/_packed_lump.py`). Two states of the optimal policy's chain that pay the same and move into every block of a partition with the same chances are one state; the partition is refined from "goal or not, and the cost paid" to its fixpoint. The graph's own merge asks for the same future under every action; the lumping only for the action the policy takes. R04's chain of 11,767 classes and 17.1 million transitions lumps to 552 blocks and 2,746 transitions, R14's 65,148 classes and 279,922 transitions to 14,743 blocks and 79,536. The chances are compared exactly (residues modulo two 31-bit primes of the weight times the inverse of the total, which also covers the kernels with weights beyond 62 bits that R04's essence rerolls have), and a final pass checks every state's float chance into every block against its block's to 1e-12, so a hash collision cannot pass. The risk of a packed plan is evaluated on the lumped chain: the same odds, mean, variance and whole cost distribution.
- **Percentiles.** R04's lumped chain is run on the coarse grid of ticket #190 (`evaluate(coarse_first=True)`: tried whenever a one-sided Chebyshev bound does not show that the ordinary grid reaches the level, not only when a lower bound shows that it cannot). R14's has 14,743 states, more than the coarse grid's dense DP takes, but it restarts: its cost is a sum of independent attempts, so the budget DP runs on one attempt and the renewal equation adds them (`risk/_regen.py`, `evaluate(regenerative=True)`). Both are certified brackets, the lower and the upper chain of a grid (a cost rounds down on one and up on the other); the report prints the middle and "certified low to high". **R04: p50 48,538 Chaos (48,082 to 48,994), p90 157,293 (155,813 to 158,773), a bracket within 1.9%; R14: p50 4,570 (4,276 to 4,865), p90 12,278 (11,391 to 13,165), a bracket 14.5% wide (±7.2%), on a step of 0.41 Chaos.** (The start price is added to both in the report.) R14's bracket is as narrow as five minutes of risk run make it on a runner that holds the 8 GB walk next to the four attempt runs; a finer step was tried and ran the runner out of memory, so the ladder ends there (`REGEN_PARTS`).
- **The report shows the lumped chain** when the policy has more than 1,000 item states (`Plan.view_policy`): each block is shown by one of its members, with its exact chances, costs and expected remaining cost; R04 prints 551 groups of its 18,696 item states, R14 14,742 of its 87,251. The text tree stops after 1,000 states and points to the JSON policy, which holds all of them. `--verify` runs the whole policy on concrete items (it reads each state's offer without building its thousand-outcome action), and a given route is read on the walked graph (`cli/_route_packed.py`): the graph restricted to the route's actions, merged, lumped and evaluated like the plan's own chain; a route rule that the game refuses falls through to the next one when the route says `skip_refused: true` (the completed routes do).
- **`benchmark --heavy` solves R04 and R14** and records the guide's route as written (it has gaps: its mean is infinite) and completed with a restart (`completed_route`, `solver_not_dearer_than_completed`).
- *Note, 2026-11-07 (#599, #628).* "Completed with a restart" is no longer what the completed route is. At the guide's gaps a last `restart` threw away a fractured mod the guide's own loop could keep (R04: 297,863.08 Chaos against 209,293.57 once the gaps are filled by that loop). ## Update: research #376, the lower bound and what it cuts

Research #376 (note `docs/research/2026-11-06-376-junk-aware-bound.md`, scripts `research/376/`, PR #501) answered point 3.

- **The bound.** The optimistic model on the key (status of the wanted mods, rarity, mods per side, fractured ids, crafted mods) is a lower bound by construction: one abstract action per abstract state and kernel at the members' cheapest cost, no outcome dropped. Measured on the walked graphs, it reaches 99.48% of the exact cost on R04 (7,791 abstract states), 98.10% on R14 (850; 99.99% with the protected tags of the junk, 1,969) and 100% on S02. The cheap bounds of point 3 were weak because their key left out the crafted mods (the metamod "Cannot roll Attack Modifiers"), not because they were blind to the junk mod types; the junk kinds add 0.1% to 1.9%. Point 3's condition, half of the exact cost on R04 and R14, is met.
- **What an exact cut does with it.** Used as the exact cut of point 4 against an incumbent, the bound keeps 12.7% (perfect upper bound) to 21.6% (incumbent) of R14's 816,153 states, close to a perfect bound's 10.7% and 20.3%. On R04 it keeps 78% to 98% of the 177,242 states: R04's actions are near ties (an error of 0.03% already keeps 78%, an inference from the measured cuts), so no buildable bound cuts it much.
- **Decision (#498).**
  - The envelope search (#377) is built as the exact cut that OR-12 names (branch-and-bound against an incumbent route: the given route or a cheap first solve), with no tolerance cut (OR-11, OR-12). It comes after the bound is built from the abstraction without the walk (#497), and is measured on R05, R13, R04 and R14. Its expected gain is R14's walk to about a fifth; R04's walk mostly stays, which is the price of an exact answer among near-equal routes. R04's page is not the walk's size: it is one step per player action (#466).
  - The bound is also the guarantee of a search that a stop-and-say-so net ends (OR-12, OR-13): the report states the cost `U` of the plan it found and the bound `LB` of the whole graph (#499). `[LB, U]` is a certificate on a point answer, not an answer given as an interval (OR-11).


## Update: #497, the bound built from the abstraction without the walk

The bound of research #376 is now built from the `Abstraction` and the priced offers alone (`abstraction/_pattern.py`, `cli/_pattern_bound.py`; note `docs/research/2026-11-06-497-walk-free-bound.md`, PR #504). Nothing walks the concrete graph.

- **How.** The key is `(flags, fractured mods, status of the wanted mods, item state, crafted slots, ordinary mods per side, and with the metamods modelled their protected tags per side)`. The members of a key are made from the key, as items the abstraction accepts. Each action is offered at the members its class stands for: one member for an erasing reroll that reads no junk; one for each relevant multiset and class composition for a bench craft, Remove Crafted Mods and the Annulment (also under protected tags, when the tags are in the key); the lightest and the heaviest junk of each class for a single-draw addition (Augmentation, Regal, Exalted; ADR-0023's extremes, extended to the protected tags); every member for the Fracturing Orb, a reroll under a lock and any action not declared one of these (sound by construction, not proved to need fewer: #508). The argument of each class is the module docstring.
- **A refusal never drops an action silently.** A game refusal (`NotApplicableError`) leaves the action out of a member. A model gap (`OutOfScopeError`) leaves it out of a key only when no member of the key can take it; otherwise the build refuses and names the offer and the key, because the exact model drops the action only where the gap is, and leaving it out of the key could put the bound above the exact cost (OR-12, OR-13).
- **Measured** (GitHub runners; the numbers are in the note). The start value is the member-union value of #376 to 1.1e-12 on R04, R14, S02, R05 and R13: R04 99.62% of the exact cost (13,060 states; 99.48% and 7,791 without the tags), R14 99.99% (1,969 states; 98.10% and 850 without), S02 99.9998% (91), R05 and R13 100%. At every walked state (R04 177,242, R14 816,153, S02 106,002, R05 78, R13 2,887) the bound at its key is at most the exact value and the key is in the model. It evaluates 79,278 of R04's 350,923 kernels, 10,615 of R14's 1,578,415 (the member-union experiment kept 307,163 and 206,626 for the extreme members) and 35 of S02's 186, and builds in one pass in 12-258 s and 0.25-2.5 GB (R14: 12-21 s and 260-300 MB).
- **Open.** A goal with a roll limit, a Divine Orb, an unveil offer or imprints has no members (#507); the Fracturing Orb and a reroll under a lock use every member (#508); declared block invariance is trusted (#509); the slot universe can miss a mod drawn from a pool wider than the base's at a member that is not offered (#520). The envelope search (#377) and the certificate `[LB, U]` (#499) are the callers.

## Update: #377, the envelope search

Ticket #377 builds point 4's exact cut on the bound of #497 (`cli/_envelope.py`, `cli/_envelope_plan.py`; `make_plan(solver="envelope")`). The measured numbers are in the pull request (#541).

- **The algorithm.** A lower graph over the states known: a tip (known, not expanded) has one fake action to the goal at its pattern bound; an expanded state has the actions built so far, and one fake action at the least lower bound of the offers not built. Each offer's lower bound is the least backup of its abstract actions at the state's key under the lowered bound values (`la(s, o) <= Q*(s, o)`; the restart is always a candidate, at `price + LB(start)`). The graph is a stochastic shortest path with restarts, solved by policy iteration to a certified residual (`solve_graph`) each round. The greedy policy of the lower graph names the tips to expand and the offers to build; an offer is built at a state only when it is the greedy action, or its bound is below the best action built there. The search stops when the greedy policy reaches no fake action.
- **The cut by the upper bound is implied.** An offer whose lower bound exceeds `V*(s)` is never greedy, since the optimal offer has `Q_L <= Q* = V*(s)`, so the search never builds it: a stronger cut than "lower bound above the best upper bound", which an executable policy (`U >= V*`) could only approximate. `U` is what a stop returns and what a given route completed with a restart (`incumbent`) gives from the first round: the restart at every tip is an executable action when the start is bought, and a given start has none (a stop before a policy closes is then an error, not a plan).
- **The mean is exact, not only the decision.** When the greedy policy reaches no tip, the lower graph restricted to its reach is the evaluation equation of that policy, so `L(root)` is its value, which is `>= V*` and `<= V*`: the mean is pinned by `LB = UB`, and every state the policy reaches is expanded. The docstring of `_envelope.py` carries the argument, `tests/test_cli_envelope.py` the check against a dense optimum on random graphs.
- **Fixed points and the moving bound.** `L` is a fixed point of the Bellman operator of the lower graph; it only rises as fake actions are replaced by real ones (the bound is consistent: it solves the model's own equation and a concrete action is no cheaper than the model's), and a run-time guard refuses a bound that falls (`BoundError`, `SearchStats.largest_fall`). Every comparison that cuts is made against values lowered by the certified relative error of the solve and by `1e-9` (rounding of the model's sums), so a cut never rests on a rounding. No tolerance cut, no lumping.
- **The net.** A time, state or memory net (`Net`, none by default) ends the search between rounds; the plan is the best executable one (realized actions, restart at every tip), `Plan.limits` says "The search was stopped" with `[LB, U]`, and nothing claims the cheapest plan. This is the seam of #499.
- **The report** carries the cut as a `Limit` of the group "How the answer was found": "The search expanded only what could matter", with the number of states looked at and actions built, and the sentence "The plan is the cheapest among the methods offered. The cut is exact: nothing was left out for being unlikely or by a tolerance."
- **Not wired by default** (until #535, see its update). `solver="auto"` still walked first; the CLI does not pass a route as the incumbent (#536) or a net (#537).
- **Measured** (heavy runs on GitHub, ubuntu, 16 GB; the table with run ids is in the pull request). The envelope's plan and mean equal the full walk's on R05, R13, R14 and R04 (the mean to 1e-12; the same action at every state of the plan, but one tie on R14 with the incumbent). Expanded: R05 9 of 78, R13 191 of 2,887, **R14 92,691 of 816,153 (11.4%)** in 51 s and 736 MB against the walk's 1,562 s and 8.4 GB (research #376: 12.7% to 21.6%, the optimal policy 10.7%), **R04 138,795 of 177,242 (78.3%)** in 5,460 s and 9.6 GB against 339 s and 3.4 GB: exact, but slower than the walk, as the note said for near ties (issue #568).

## Update: #539, the guard of the bound runs as the search goes

The guard of #377 judged the bound once, when the run had ended; a bound above the exact value could make the lower graph unsolvable before that (R13, every third or fifth key raised 20 or 50 times: "the policy is improper: its linear system is singular" or "certifies only a relative bound", after 60 rounds in the first case, in 3 of 4 plants; the fourth was the fall guard). The failure itself is the solver's: a policy of more than 1,500 states is evaluated by BiCGSTAB, whose reported residual is the recurrence's and not the true one, and on a stiff system it returned values with a true residual of 1e17; the improvement step took them for real and chose a policy with a closed class (issue #584; fixed since by returning the true residual `|b - A x| / |b|` from `_bicgstab`: an evaluation that does not reach it is solved densely or refused, PR #615). The planted bound only provokes it: the raised keys make the lower graph swing (`L(root)` from 6,555 to 70,430) and its first policy cost 6e5 against a cheapest action of 0.0058.

- **The guard is two checks inside `Search`** (`guard=True`, `BoundError` now in `_envelope.py`): when a state is expanded, its bound must not be above the least `la` of its offers by more than `CONSISTENCY_LIMIT` (1e-5; a consistent bound solves the model's own equation, so a state's bound is at most the cheapest backup of its offers, and a raised key breaks this at once since the cost term of the backup is not raised); after each round, `L(root)` must not have fallen by more than `FALL_LIMIT`. Three of the four plants are refused at their first raised key that is expanded; the fourth (every 3rd key raised 50 times) is refused at round 2 by the check of the unexpanded tips after the lower graph's solve fails, instead of after minutes.
- **A `ConvergenceError` stays one** unless the bound has shown a fault first. A sound bound reaches it too: a free action (cost 0), or a cost ratio of 1e14 between the cheapest action and the values, cannot be certified even on a perfect bound (issue #585). Nothing is caught: the guard raises before the solve it would have spoiled.

## Update: #568, the rounds of the envelope search no longer solve the lower graph from scratch

R04's envelope search was exact but slow (48 rounds, 5,460 s, 9.6 GB against the walk's 339 s and 3.4 GB). Profile (heavy runs 42316164749, and 42330898955 on the code before the fix, which stopped at 2,400 s in round 34): about 98% of the time was `solve_graph`, and the cause was not the number of rounds but what a round's solve was made of. A graph of 138,809 nodes stored **1.05 million edges** (a kernel, the successors of an offer, is shared by every state of a key) but the solver walked **158 million** (every state's own copy of its kernel) in each of ten to eleven policy-iteration steps of a round, in the product of BiCGSTAB and in the Q of every action. Each round also started its policy iteration from the cheapest path and joined all kernels into new arrays.

The fix is in the solver and the search, not in the route:

- **The solver sums each kernel once** (`Work.shared_sums`, `evaluate_policy`). The sum over a kernel is shared by all actions that use it; an action whose kernel holds its own node has that term taken off (`self_mass`, which the search knows when it builds the kernel). An action whose own term is above 1/2 of the kernel, or whose kernel has fewer than 64 edges, keeps its edge-by-edge sum (`Work.edgewise`): taking a large term off a sum loses digits. The values equal the old ones to 1e-15 on a synthetic graph shaped like R04's (cold solve 15.7 s to 2.2 s, same policy). This also speeds the full walk's solve and removes the per-edge temporaries that were most of the 9.6 GB.
- **The kernels live in flat arrays** (`Kernels`): every edge once, a round's graph is a view; no second copy, no chances worked out again.
- **Each round starts from the last round's policy and values** (`Search._warm`, `solve_graph(start_policy=, start_value=)`). The ticket's worry stands: a mix of the previous policy and the cheapest-path one is not proved proper, and a state whose fake action is gone or whose action now leads back has no good previous action. `warm_policy` therefore fills a missing action by the cheapest under the last values, and then proves the policy proper by the finite chain's test (every state reaches the goal with positive chance, which for a finite chain is properness): if it holds at every state with an action, the start is kept and no search for the proper set is made; if not, the proper set is found as before and the policy is kept at the states that reach the goal and replaced by the cheapest path's action at the others. That result is proper (a state that kept its action reaches the goal by states that kept theirs; a replaced state follows the strictly falling cheapest-path cost until it meets one), so the fallback is a proof, not a hope. The answer does not depend on the start.

**Measured** (heavy runs on GitHub, ubuntu): R04 exact, 48 rounds, same expanded states (138,795 of 177,242) and realized actions (618,826) as before; solve time of all rounds 27 s (the old code: 2,239 s by round 34 of 48, run 42330898955), search resident memory 1.2 GB (was 9.6 GB); wall 394 s against the full walk's 384 s in the same session (run 42334113161 against 42334097295), mean 69257.53649803626 against 69257.53649797948 (8e-13), the same action at all 18,696 states of the plan. R14: 64 s and 750 MB (was 70 s and 759 MB on the same day; run 42334128974 against 42332256238), mean 5784.488692220995 against 5784.488692220736. What is left of R04's time is not the solve: the bound's build (74 to 140 s by runner), the expansion of 138,795 states (332 s, the abstraction's `kernel_key` and kernel work, as much per action as the walk's), and the post-processing of the plan (`flow_limits`, 13 s and a 4.7 GB spike that the full walk has too).

## Update: #535, `auto` is the envelope search wherever its bound can be built

`make_plan(solver="auto")`, the default of the command and of every caller, is the envelope search of #377 whenever `abstraction.pattern_refusal` says the bound of #497 can be built, and the command has `--solver {auto,object,packed,envelope}`. The check is one function, `pattern_refusal(abstraction)` (`abstraction/_pattern.py`): `Pattern.__init__` raises its text as `OutOfScopeError`, and `make_plan` asks it before any solve, so the choice is made before anything runs. Where it refuses (a goal with a roll limit, an action that rerolls values, an unveil offer, imprints: #507) `auto` is the path it was: the budget decision, `solve_pruned` for 40 offers or more, otherwise the walk on packed arrays with the object solver for graphs of at most `EXACT_TRANSITIONS`.

- **No fallback once the search has started.** A `NoPolicy`, `BoundError` or `ConvergenceError` from the envelope search under `auto` is a `PlanError`, as with `solver="envelope"`; its text says the cause and names `--solver packed`. The player chooses the walk; the planner never swaps to it behind an answer.
- **With 40 offers or more `auto` keeps the bound pruning, for now.** At `MIN_OFFERS_TO_PRUNE` (the fossil sets) `auto` is `solve_pruned`, as before. On fossil-set worlds the envelope search is 1.2 to 40 times slower than the bound pruning (#696; #742: a boots world with 120 fossil sets ran past 20 minutes where the pruning takes seconds) and refuses R09 (#731). - **A plan a given route is judged against walks** (`make_plan(..., judges_route=True)`: the command with `--route`, `benchmark` for a route with a guide, the scripts that judge routes). ## Update: #826, an executable plan as the upper side, and the stop at a proved 1% (OR-32)

OR-32 (owner, 2026-11-13, #822) replaced OR-11: a plan proved at most 1% above the cheapest, its mean exact. OR-39 (the retro of the same day, #900) narrows it: a plan is shown as soon as an executable one exists and the proof stops by itself at a proved 1%, whatever the time; the minute of OR-32 is the time a plan aims at (OR-33) and a default of the machine's setting (OR-40), not the switch between two kinds of answer. The ticket's text still had the minute as that switch; it is built to OR-39. This update amends three earlier statements of this ADR and one of ADR-0023:

- **Point 4 of the decision and the decision of #498 ("with no tolerance cut").** Every cut of the search stays exact: no action and no state is left out by a tolerance, a chance or a count. What changed is when the proof ends. The search now may stop before the root is proved, at a stated gap between an executable plan's exact cost and the lower figure. That is a tolerance on the proof, written beside the answer (OR-13), not a cut.
- **"Update: #535", "`budget` does not apply to the envelope search: it is exact whatever the size ... no answer is approximate (OR-11)".** The budget applies: its `gap` is the gap of the stop, from the first round. With no budget, `Budget.unlimited()` or a gap of 0 the search is exact whatever the size.
- **"Update: #377", the upper bound as "what a stop returns".** The upper side is now part of every search, from before its first round.

What was built (`planner/_envelope.py`, `planner/_envelope_plan.py`, `planner/_plan.py`, `budget.py`):

- **The first plan.** Before the first round the search realizes one action along a chain from the start: the offer with the least lower cost `la` at each state (a refused offer passes to the next), or the offers a given route names. That chain, with the restart at every state it leaves when the start item is bought, is an executable plan; its cost, solved with a certified residual, is the first upper figure `U`. The research measured it on R04 (the optimum itself, 12 s, 15,003 states) and R14 (0.0004% above the optimum, 37 s).
- **The upper side as the search goes.** `U` is the value of the best policy of the upper graph: every realized action, plus the restart at every state but the start. Every action in it is a real action with its exact kernel, so `U` is the exact cost of a plan that can be executed; the graph only gains actions, so `U` only falls. A given start item has no restart: `U` is infinite until a policy of the realized actions closes, which the first plan usually does.
- **The stop.** Under a budget with a gap, whatever the time (OR-39; the search itself can be told to wait some seconds, `gap_after`, which the planner sets to 0), the search ends at the first round where `U (1 + its certified error) <= (1 + gap) L(root) (1 - its certified error)`. Then `mean(plan) <= U <= (1 + gap) L(root) <= (1 + gap) V*`. The argument needs exactly two things: `L(root)` is a lower bound of the cheapest plan (the bound of #497 and the lower graph of #377), and `U` is the exact cost of the plan returned. `tests/test_envelope_gap_stop.py` works three small worlds by hand and breaks each condition once (an upper figure that is a guess; a lower figure above the optimum): each control ends on a plan outside its gap.
- **A round whose upper solve cannot be certified** (the refusals of #769: R04's round-4 plan certified only 4.7e-4 in the research run) does not stop: the stop waits for a certified `U`, and the answer says in how many rounds it waited.
- **A net's stop on a plan that cannot be certified** returns the last plan that could (the first plan, or an earlier round's), over the states that plan knew, and says so; it refused before (OR-33). The search keeps that plan only when a net has a limit.
- **The answer.** The plan the search ended with is solved again exactly (`solve_walked`), and that mean is the one shown. `EnvelopeInfo.within` is the gap when the search ended at it; the limit of kind "How the answer was found" says that the search stopped once the plan was proved close to the cheapest, the plan's exact mean, the figure no plan can cost less than, and the gap actually proved (`envelope_limits`, `certificate_text`). Every other limit's claim of "the cheapest" reads "within the stated gap of the cheapest" (`Plan.__post_init__`), for a search a net stopped too.
- **Read while it runs.** `Search.standing` and the `watch` callback (`make_plan(watch=...)`, `PlanSettings.watch`) give the best plan's cost so far and the lower figure after the first plan and after each round; `Search.plan_now()` returns that plan. What the website needs on top (the plan as a route page while the proof goes on, a stop the user asks for) is #906, not built here.
- **`eps` is a setting**: `budget.PROOF_GAP` (1%), `Budget.gap`, `--gap PERCENT`. The aim and the net are settings of the machine the run is on (`Budget.aim_seconds`, `Budget.seconds`), never constants sized for a runner.
- **What it does not do.** It does not choose what to expand by the gap (#836), and it does not make the lower bound tighter: where the bound starts far below the optimum (a generated `basic-orbs/small` target: 90.3% of it) the stop at 1% fires only after the rounds have raised `L(root)` to within 1%, which is most of the proof. The figure that decides how much proof the stop saves is the bound's error at the start.

## Update: #769, the certificate measures a free state against the cost ahead

The certificate of #374 divided each state's residual by `g(s)`, the cost of its cheapest action. A state whose actions are all free (an unveil, a pick: ADR-0026, priced at `FREE_ACTION_SHARE`, 1e-6 of the cheapest paid offer) has a `g` far below the rounding of its own sum, `(m + 2) eps V`, so R24's plan was refused at 1.5e-3 however good its values were (heavy run 43255914334), and a free action of cost exactly 0 could never be certified (#585).

- **The argument.** For any `h >= 0` that is 0 at the goals and outside the solved states, `gamma = T h - h` is a margin every action of a state pays, so `(I - P_nu)^-1 gamma <= V^nu` for every proper policy; a residual `d <= t gamma` gives `V* >= V / (1 + t)`. On the upper side `kappa = T_mu h - h` of the greedy policy, with `e < kappa` everywhere, proves that policy proper and gives `V^mu <= V / (1 - max e / kappa)`. `gamma` and `kappa` are lowered by the rounding of their sums and of the subtraction. `h = 0` is the certificate of #374 (`gamma = g`, `kappa = k`).
- **The cost ahead.** `h = (w / L)` times the sum of the cheapest `j`-step costs `C_j = T^j 0`, `j = 1..L`, `w = 1/2`. Since `c_a + P_a C_j >= C_(j+1)`, the margin is at least `(1 - w) c_a + (w / L) (C_(L+1) - g)`: at a free state the paid step after it, at a paid state at least half its own cost. Each side takes the better of `h = 0` and the cost ahead.
- **When and how far.** The cost ahead is measured only when `h = 0` does not give the accepted bound (1e-6), so every plan that certified before keeps its numbers. The window starts at 2 steps and doubles while the bound is above the accepted one and the doubling does not make it worse than twice the last; a run of free steps priced at an epsilon gives every window the same margin until the window reaches the paid step after it. `AHEAD_LIMIT` (64 steps) is a stop-and-say-so rail on its cost (OR-12), named in the refusal.
- **What it gives.** On an unveil of 10^3 to 10^5 outcomes onto choice nodes that pick between paid states of values 1e2 to 1e6, with the free actions at their epsilon or at exactly 0, the certificate holds the values of exact `Fraction` elimination and is within 4 times the rounding of the longest sum times the Skeel condition of the plan (`tests/test_solver_packed_free_certificate.py`); on those graphs the cheapest-cost measure gave 4e-5 (refused). What it cannot certify is a free cycle (a run kept from the goals forever at no cost: no residual bounds the values from below), which stays a `ConvergenceError` (`tests/test_cli_envelope_guard.py`).
