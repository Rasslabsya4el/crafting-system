# An approximate answer is an executable policy between two certified bounds

When the exact compressed state is too large to solve in about a minute, the planner solves two smaller models of a **coarse state** instead of one big one, and reports a policy that it can run on a real item together with an interval that provably holds the exact mean cost. The answer is marked **approximate** and its error is the certified gap, never a guess. This is the "approximate merges and their error measurement" of the map; the owner's tolerance is [#81](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/81) Q2: exact when a benchmark query fits in about a minute, past that an approximate answer whose measured error is at most 1% of the mean cost. It amends ADR-0009 ("v1 allows only exact merges"): an approximate merge is allowed, but only in this form.

## The two models and the gap

A **coarse state** is a partition of the exact compressed states into blocks (ADR-0009's states with some slot kinds renamed to a coarser kind). Two models are solved on the blocks, each taken **per member**: for every member of a block and every action, the member's exact kernel is pushed through the block map.

- the **optimistic model** offers, in a block, every action that is legal in at least one member and charges the cheapest member. Its value `V-` of a block is at most the exact optimum `V*` of every member (the restriction of `V*` to a block is a supersolution of this operator, and value iteration from zero rises to its least fixed point);
- the **pessimistic model** offers an action only if it is legal in every member and charges the worst member. Its best policy `pi`, read on the members, is an ordinary policy of the exact problem, and its value `V+` is at least `pi`'s exact cost `J(pi)`.

So `V-(B) <= V*(s) <= J(pi)(s) <= V+(B)` for every member `s` of `B`. The answer shows `pi` and the mean `V+`, and the **certified gap** `V+/V- - 1`. Because `V*` lies in the interval too, the gap bounds the true error of the policy shown, `J(pi)/V* - 1 <= V+/V- - 1`, and bounds the error of the mean it reports the same way. It meets the tolerance when it is at most 1%. (Checked against brute force on 300 random models, 1,800 state checks, largest violation 4e-15, by the `checker` of PR #121.)

The solver's own error enters the gap. `solve` returns values with `V(1-e) <= V* <= V(1+e)` (`Solution.error_bound`, ADR-0010), so the reported lower bound is `V-(1-e-)` and the upper `V+(1+e+)`; the gap is `V+(1+e+) / (V-(1-e-)) - 1`. A table in this ADR is **certified** only when its numbers come from `solve` with this correction; the numbers from the prototype's numpy value iteration are called measured.

### Per-member, never from one representative

A block may be built from one representative member only for an action whose kernel, pushed through the block map, is the same for all members (**block-invariant**). Otherwise the bound is false. Example: a block `{s1, s2}`, `s1` reaches the goal at cost 1, `s2` loops with probability 0.9 (true cost 10); the representative `s1` gives an "upper" bound of 1. The actions that are block-invariant when only slots without a requirement are renamed:

- an erasing reroll: Chaos Orb, Harvest reforge, essences and **fossils in a resonator** (`ApplyFossils` is in `ERASING` of `abstraction/_kinds.py`, ADR-0009): the outcome depends on the flags, the fractured mods and the action, which a block keeps whole. This covers the 420-pool Tangled sets of [#118](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/118);
- a bench craft and Remove Crafted Mods: deterministic, the successor block is a function of the block and the craft; only legality differs between members.

For these the models differ only in which actions are offered, so both are ordinary models and need no change in the solver. The build proves block-invariance for each action class it supports (a test compares every member of every block on the fixtures, tolerance 1e-9; the prototype did it on all 13,172 members of R05 and all 386 of the harder route below, largest difference 0). **Every other action refuses the coarse state until a ticket proves its invariance.** The reasons differ: an addition that keeps the mods (Exalted, Regal, Augmentation; Alteration and Transmutation erase the mods like the other rerolls and are already block-invariant) draws from a pool whose total weight depends on which families the item holds, so members whose pooled families have different weights give different kernels (pooling only families of equal weight is exact and is already ADR-0009's family type; pooling families of different weights is covered by variants, below); Annulment chooses uniformly among the removable mods (`docs/research/2026-10-30-2-state-compression.md`) and **is block-invariant** (settled in [#130](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/130), below): a pool of `n` slots loses one with probability `n / N` whatever families they are, so the pushed kernel is the same for every member; its `Reads` declares it since [#172](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/172); the recombinator, imprint and Awakener's Orb of ADR-0012/0022 read the mods of two items and are unanalysed. A query that enables a refused action has no family-group level: it is exact or ends with "no approximate level available". The approximate level covers queries made of erasing rerolls, bench crafts, Remove Crafted Mods, Annulment and, by variants, additions (measured below; as built in #172).

### When the members differ: variants (settled in #130)

A model whose successor distribution depends on the member makes the pessimistic backup a max over members (variants), i.e. a min-max game, and the optimistic backup a min over them. ADR-0010's certificate survives it. It uses only `T_mu(aV) = a T_mu V + (1-a) k_mu` with `k_mu` the constant term of the policy (cost plus the fixed downstream part) and a lower bound `g` on all `k`. For the operator `T+_mu V = max_v (c + P_v V + out_v)`, each variant satisfies the identity with its own `k_v`, so for `a >= 1` `T+_mu(aV) <= a T+_mu V - (a-1) g` and for `a <= 1` `T+_mu(aV) >= a T+_mu V + (1-a) g` with `g` the smallest constant term over all actions and variants; the multipliers `g/(g-e)` and `g/(g+d)` and the supersolution argument are unchanged.

**The extremes.** An adding action that keeps the mods (Augmentation, Regal, Exalted) draws a mod from the pool minus the families the item holds. Pool the junk families into `p` pools; let `H_j` be the weight of the families of pool `j` that a member holds. Every outcome probability is then `(c_0 + sum_j c_j H_j) / (W - sum_j H_j)` with common denominator `W - sum H_j` (`W` the pool total), so the backup of any continuation values, summed over the outcomes that land in one successor block, is linear-fractional in `(H_1..H_p)`. A linear-fractional function is quasi-convex and quasi-concave, so over the box that the members' `H` fill it is largest and smallest at a vertex, and a vertex is a member (the pools are independent): the lightest or the heaviest member of each pool. The **variants** of a block are these `2^p` members, `p` the pools that hold slots; the optimistic backup takes the min and the pessimistic the max over them, and nothing else is enumerated. The successor block does not depend on the member, so the same block values serve all variants. The erasing actions, Scouring and Annulment are block-invariant, so their pushed kernel is the same on every variant (the prototype asserts it on the lightest and heaviest member of every block it builds; the argument above and the uniform choice of Annulment cover the rest).

**The oracle** (required before a solver change lands; `research/130/oracle.py` on the branch `research/130-prototype`). (A) 400 random min-max models (random states, actions, variant kernels): the value of the optimistic and the pessimistic model against brute force over every stationary policy and every choice of the variant: worst relative difference 3.4e-14 and 6.5e-14, certified bound over brute-force value at most 2.6e-13, and the identity `T(aV) <= aT(V) - (a-1)g` (and its `a <= 1` form) with no violation in 4,000 checks. (C) The extremes claim on the real Hunter Thicket Bow pools: 300 random pools and blocks, every member enumerated, 1,776 random continuation values: worst difference 3.6e-15. The same oracle ran on the GitHub runners (run 41251657124) with the same numbers. Oracle (A) checks the models and the identity on small games; the tables below were solved by the prototype's nested policy iteration, and rest on its own residual certificate, not on the oracle. The raw logs live on the research branch; the oracle is a test since #172 (`tests/test_solver_minmax.py`, below).

## Which merges are allowed

1. **Family groups: pool the families no requirement cares about.** A junk family is told apart in ADR-0009 only because an enabled bench craft would clash with it. Families whose mods give no requirement, and that no goal-relevant craft clashes with, are pooled into groups; relevant ones stay apart, and status, counts and sides are never merged. A junk mod with several families (`held_families`) is never pooled: its kind stays its family key, as does any crafted mod with a requirement. Fractured mods, told-apart junk and told-apart crafted mods hold their families exactly; let `H` be the families of a group `g` of `k` families that they hold. A craft of family `phi` in `g` is illegal in both models if `phi` is in `H` (the fractured mod's family included, as in R05's fractured All Attributes); otherwise, with `n` pooled slots of `g` on the item, it is legal in the optimistic model when `n <= k - |H| - 1` and in the pessimistic model only when `n = 0`. A crafted mod with no requirement is pooled in the same way. Only for the block-invariant actions above.
2. **Tail cut: expand exactly only where the policy goes.** Following a coarse policy, states with an expected number of entries (visits, without the repeats of a self-loop; see "How the tail cut is built") not above `theta` are not expanded; a cut successor takes the optimistic and the pessimistic value of its block as its two leaf values. As tight as the leaf bounds, so it needs a coarse model to start from. With variants (#130) it removes the gap that the leaves leave only down to a floor, and costs about what a finer coarse level costs (table below); on the measured route it looks the cheaper rung when the coarse level is just above 1% (one case, the cheap prices below; the cost of finding the states is not measured).
3. **Weight buckets: not now, and never better than variants.** Rounding pool weights to a geometric grid gives a bound by an interval kernel (nature picks the best or worst distribution inside `[p / r^4, p r^4]` at each backup, the variants argument above), but it is loose: 1.2% already at 5% buckets. The interval polytope contains the member kernels of the same pooling, so for that pooling it is never tighter than variants, which take the members themselves.

Not allowed: any merge that changes what an action can do or cost on the goal-relevant part of the state, or that lacks both models.

## How the coarse state is built (ticket #125)

`abstraction.Coarsening(abstraction, groups)` is the family-group level. A **block** is a `CompressedState` whose pooled slots have the kind `=g<i>`; `coarsen(state)` is the map and `kernel(block, action, bound)` the kernel of an action in the optimistic (`"lower"`) or pessimistic (`"upper"`) model, taken per member and pushed through `coarsen`.

- **What is pooled.** Only families that an enabled action tells apart (the families of the crafts it reads), one name per family, that no requirement names (an ordinary mod of the family in a leaf: refused with `ValueError`). `pooling_irrelevant(abstraction, size)` also leaves out the families a craft that gives a requirement clashes with (the blockers of the goal craft) and cuts the rest, sorted by name, into groups of `size`. A group may list families of both sides; its blocks pool them per side, so `k` is the number of the group's families on the side of the slot. The groups of 8 and 4 of the measurement below are cut this way (the same block counts: 365, 1,582, 4,295 on R05).
- **What stays exact.** A junk mod with several families (kind = its family key), a hybrid crafted mod, fractured mods, a crafted mod whose family is not pooled, and slots whose kind is already `""`. They hold their families in every member: `H` of the rule above is read from them.
- **Legality is read from the action, not from a rule of the bench.** The members of a block are the ways to put its `n` pooled slots on distinct families of the pool outside `H` (a pooled crafted slot on a family that has a crafted mod). For an action the builder runs the exact kernel on one representative member for each way the families in `Reads.families` can be held, and the other families of the pool stand for each other; the lower model offers the action if one representative can take it, the upper only if all can. For a bench craft that is the rule above (`n <= k - |H| - 1` and `n = 0`, illegal in both when its family is in `H`), and a Remove Crafted Mods or an erasing reroll (no family read) has one representative. A new method declares `Reads(families=...)` and `block_invariant`; nothing in `abstraction` names it. An enabled action that is not declared block-invariant, reads every family, or adds mods and keeps the junk (`Reads.adding()`, `pool_weight`: its kernel depends on the pool weight of the families held, and pooling families of different weights changes it) makes the constructor raise `OutOfScopeError`; an action asked for a kernel that is not declared raises it too. A limit, not a decision: pooling only families of equal weight is exact for an adding action and is already the family type of ADR-0009. [#130](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/130) lifts it by variants (above): the build ticket gives an adding action's coarse kernel as the per-member extremes of its pools.
- **A gap needs a family that can be held instead.** Pooling the two blockers of one hybrid craft with nothing else loses nothing (every member holds one of them, so the craft is blocked in all); pooling a blocker with another family does (a member holds the other, so the craft is open in one model only). The committed fixture shows both.

## How the tail cut is built (ticket #129)

`solver.solve_tail_cut(exact, optimistic, pessimistic, block_of, start, theta)` takes the exact model, the two coarse models and the map from exact states to blocks (`Coarsening.coarsen`); the solver stays generic, so it knows no game. It solves the coarse pair from the block of the start (`solve_bounds`), follows the coarse policy `pi` in the exact model and **expands** every state it enters with an expected count above `theta`: such a state keeps all its exact actions. Every other successor is a **leaf**, a state of the tail model with one action to the goal that costs the lower bound of its block in the optimistic tail model and the upper bound of its block in the pessimistic one. `solve_bounds` then runs on the two tail models.

- **Why the bounds hold.** The optimum of a leaf is at least the lower bound of its block, so the exact optimum is a supersolution of the optimistic tail operator and the tail value is below it. For the upper bound let `G(s) = U(block(s))`. `G` is above the backup of `pi` in every state, so it is above the pessimistic tail operator on the expanded states and equals the leaf value on the leaves: the tail value `W` is at most `G` there. The policy `mu` that plays the tail action in an expanded state and `pi` in every other state has the cost `J(mu)` at most `Z`, with `Z = W` on the expanded states and `Z = G` elsewhere (for an expanded state `Z` is its tail backup; for any other state `G` is above the backup of `pi` over `G`, which is above the backup over `Z <= G`), because a supersolution of a policy's own backup bounds its cost. So `V* <= J(mu) <= W`, and `mu` is what `bounds.policy` and `coarse.policy` together give. A block bound is a sub- or supersolution of the exact backup, so the tail bounds are never looser than the coarse ones and tighten as the expanded set grows; with nothing expanded (`theta` of 1 or more, or `math.inf`) they are the coarse bounds. Any expanded set gives valid bounds: `theta` only decides how tight they are and what is solved. `tests/test_solver_tail.py` checks `lower <= V* <= J(mu) <= upper` in exact fractions against brute force on 120 random block-structured models, for every start and nine values of `theta`; `tests/test_abstraction_coarse_tail.py` does the same on the committed fixture, comparing with `solve` on the exact graph and on the policy the cut yields.
- **What `theta` counts.** The expected number of times `pi` **enters** a state from another state (the start counts as one), found by pushing probability mass along `pi` through the expanded states only, so it is a lower bound of the true count, and it is strict ("above"). The repeats of a self-loop are folded into the state's own successors and are not counted, because an unexpanded state's loop is unknown without expanding it. With `theta = 0` every state `pi` can reach is expanded. Mass below a thousandth of `theta` waiting in an expanded state is not pushed on. The ticket's "equal the coarse bounds at `theta = 0`" cannot hold for this rule (`theta = 0` expands the most): they are equal when nothing is expanded, and the test uses `math.inf` for that case.
- **Blocks the first solve did not reach.** The pessimistic model offers fewer actions than the exact one, so an exact action it does not offer can lead to a block that the pessimistic solve never meets (it has no upper bound there). Those blocks are added as starts and both models are solved again, which leaves the earlier numbers as they were or tighter. A start whose block has no finite upper bound (the pessimistic model cannot reach the goal, as when the two blockers of the fixture are pooled each with another family) is refused: there is no policy to follow.
- **Labels.** An action of the coarse policy is found in the exact model by its label, which must name one action in a state (141 ring crafts of R05 share 80 display texts, so a caller labels an offer by its craft, not its text). The pessimistic coarse model may charge more than the exact one; nothing needs the costs to be equal.
- **On the ladder** (`cli/_decide.py`, `cli/_approx.py`): after the coarse levels come three tail-cut rungs, `theta` 1e-2, 1e-3 and 1e-4, each built on the finest coarse level whose own forecast fits the time and given that forecast (with no coarse level that fits there is no tail cut). `climb` solves a rung with `solve_tail_cut` over the models it already built for the coarse levels, and the answer is marked like a coarse one, with the number of exact states expanded added to the text and the JSON (`expanded`). The policy it returns has an action for each expanded exact state and for each block the cut states fall in, so it can be walked from the start item. **Not forecast**: the time of a tail rung. The expanded states build the exact kernels of every offered action (about 1 s each on R05 under load, 581 states at 1e-4), which the forecast of the coarse level under it does not count; a rung that does not converge, or whose coarse level has no policy, is skipped (`NoPolicyError`), and any other mismatch between the models is an error.
- **The result** is a `Bounds` over the expanded states (their policy actions have exact states as outcomes), the set expanded, the number of leaves and the coarse bounds the leaves were valued by; a state outside the set follows `coarse.policy`.

A rule by the ratio of weights instead of the spread is worse and not monotone (ratio within 1.75 of the lightest: 5.6% at 7,808 blocks, within 1.5: 1.25% at 11,616 blocks, against 0.81% at 7,808 for spread 1000), so the ladder is by spread. The lifted policy was lifted only on the default prices, where its cost equals `V*` to 1e-9 (inside the 3e-6 bracket).

**Sensitivity.** With cheap Annulment and Exalted (regal 0.5, annul 2, exalt 2, scour 0.5; exact `V* = 3502.62`, a 491 s solve on a runner) spread 3000/2000/1500/1000/500 gives 2.33/2.16/1.36/**1.03**/0.21%: 1000 misses by 0.03 points and 500 (26,801 blocks) passes. With a heavier goal (Physical damage 7000, critical multiplier 6000, T1 attack speed; exact 129,138 states, `V* = 625.82`): one pool per side 22.2%, then spread 3000/2000/1500/1000/500 gives 2.29/2.11/1.32/**0.90**/0.24%, 5,863 blocks at spread 1000.

**Tail cut on top of the variants** (leaves bounded by the block values; expanded = exact states with at least `theta` expected visits under the lifted policy; the policy reaches 29,344 exact states in all, 28,504 of them not goals; the expanded counts are taken from the latter):

| Coarse level | Gap before | `theta` 1e-3 (8,065 expanded) | `theta` 1e-4 (17,006) | `theta` 1e-5 (24,680) | Floor (`theta` 1e-7, 28,477) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| one pool per side | 20.8% | 14.2% | 7.48% | 6.13% | 6.05% |
| spread <= 2000 | 1.81% | 1.49% | **0.97%** | 0.79% | 0.78% |
| spread <= 1500 | 1.11% | 0.90% | 0.58% | 0.46% | 0.45% |
| spread <= 1000 | 0.81% | 0.65% | 0.41% | 0.32% | 0.31% |

The upper bound converges to `V*`; the lower bound stops at the optimistic value of the leaves, which is the floor (6447.7 for one pool, 6816.24 for spread 1000, against 6837.52). A tail cut cannot rescue a coarse level whose own lower bound is far off (one pool per side stays at 6%), and where it works (spread 2000 reaches 0.97%) it expands 17,006 exact states and about 40,000 leaves, 160,000 edges, which is no cheaper than the spread 1000 level alone (7,808 blocks, 128,666 edges). These counts are the expanded states only: the prototype finds them by solving the expected visits over all the 28,504 non-goal exact states the lifted policy reaches (35,844 with the cheap prices), so the cost of finding the states is not in them and a real tail cut needs its own way to find them. With the cheap prices, where spread 1000 misses (1.03%), a tail cut on it reaches 1% at `theta` 0.01 (6,479 expanded states, 0.997%) and 0.85% at 1e-3 (15,914), against the 26,801 blocks of spread 500: there the tail cut looks the cheaper next rung (floor 0.41%, lower leaves 3488.38 against `V*` 3502.62), but that is one case and its search cost is not measured. The ladder rule below therefore does not include it; [#129](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/129) measures the tail cut on its own.

**A smaller craft list is not an approximation.** Exact weight-sum graph without Augmentation: 134,821 states (no change); without Annulment: 134,794 states, 869,195 edges, `V* = 8846.24` (+29%); only Transmutation, Alteration, Regal, Exalted and Scouring: `V* = 10892.34` (+59%). On the weight-sum graph the state count does not fall (the 3+3 rare slots are reachable by Regal and Exalted alone) and the answer changes, so the list is a restriction the user chooses, offered when no level fits, not a level.

**What this does and does not settle.** On this one route and three price/goal sets, variants with extremes reach a certified gap of 1% or less, with a spread of 1000 (of 59,000 per side) or 500 under cheap prices. It is evidence for the ladder below, not a guarantee: the metamods, the multimod and the bench crafts of R14 and all of R04 are unmeasured, and the first build ticket must re-measure on them. If a route misses 1% on the whole ladder (spread 500 or when the level's pilot count no longer fits), the planner says that no level reaches 1% and prints the best interval it has, as already specified for the CLI below.

## How the CLI decides, and how it marks the result

Before solving, the CLI runs a **pilot count**: one cached kernel of the first random action (1 s for the essence of R05, 2,061 outcomes), then the crafted slots that fit each outcome by sets of families and free slots, with no further kernel call. On R05 it gives 76 states for 32 crafts and 13,172 for 141, equal to the exact counts, in 0.3 s and 1.1 s, and predicts 27.1 million transitions (23.0 million built, 18% over). The #59 formula (rarity times crafts times junk configurations) overestimates this route by two orders of magnitude (1.2 million states) and stays only a cheap upper bound for targets the pilot cannot count yet.

The predicted time is `transitions * (r_kernel + r_solve)`: `r_kernel` is measured in the pilot (the cost per outcome of a second, cached kernel call: 38 microseconds on a loaded machine; the build took 906 s / 23.0 million = 39 microseconds per transition including everything), `r_solve` is the seconds per edge of `solve`, measured by a short calibration solve on the pilot's own first layers (about 7 microseconds in the M3 run). Both rates are parameters of the decision function, so that a test injects them and no test depends on a machine (ADR-0014). For R05 with 141 crafts the prediction is about 1,200 s against 906 s built plus an estimated 160 s of solving. The pilot's "within 18%" and the solve rate come from one route (R05) and from models of up to 2.1 million edges; they are not a general guarantee.

The rule: exact if the predicted time fits about a minute, otherwise the **ladder**: M1, then smaller groups of the irrelevant families, then a tail cut with M1's bounds as leaves (a tail cut needs a coarse model for its leaves, so it only follows a coarse level), stopping at the first level whose certified gap is at most 1%; a level is built only if its own pilot count fits. For a query with adding actions or Annulment (variants, #130) the junk families are pooled by weight spread instead: use the rungs spread 3000, 2000, 1500, 1000, 500 (weight units, measured on a pool of 34 families of about 59,000 weight per side; how the rungs scale to a bigger or lighter pool is not measured, and the build ticket must say how it picks them), stopping at the first rung whose certified gap is at most 1%; each level is built only if its own pilot count fits. The pilot's cost model counts one kernel call per transition; a variants block needs up to `2^p` kernel calls for each adding action, and the prototype's build took about 15-18 s for 7,808 blocks against 85 s for the exact graph, so the build ticket measures the rate again. When a refused action leaves no coarse level, the query is exact or ends with "no approximate level available". When no level reaches 1% inside the time, the planner prints the best interval it has and says that the gap is above 1%; it never rounds a gap down. It offers the exact run only when the predicted transitions are at most 2 million, the largest size the product `solve` has been run on here (30 s, memory not measured); beyond that it says that the exact run is not offered until ticket B has measured memory at larger sizes, and offers a smaller craft list.

The result carries the words "approximate (certified within X% of the exact mean)", the two bounds, the block count and the level; in the JSON `approximate: true`, `lower`, `upper`, `gap`, `level`.

### As built (ticket #127)

- `Abstraction.estimate(starts, actions)` is the pilot count. It walks the graph the way `solve` does (a goal is not expanded) but runs the real kernel once per (reroll, key), the key being the flags, the fractured mods and, for a reroll that erases crafted mods, only which metamods are held (a crafted metamod makes a reroll refuse), and decides bench crafts and Remove Crafted Mods itself by sets of families and free slots. A property test compares its states and transitions with the real expansion on random craft subsets and starts. An action of another shape (an addition, say) raises `EstimateRefused` and the query is built exactly, without a forecast. `Estimate.coarsened(coarsening)` forecasts a coarse level from the same states (the blocks are the images of the reached states; a forecast, not a bound).
- `cli._decide.choose(estimate, r_kernel, r_solve, budget, levels=, refusal=)` reads no clock. Predicted time is kernel outcomes times `r_kernel` plus solved edges times `r_solve` (for a coarse level two models are solved). Exact when it fits `--budget` (60 s by default); otherwise the levels of `LADDER` whose own forecast fits; with a `refusal` (an offered action that does not declare `Reads.block_invariant`) or no level that fits, the query is exact if it is at most 2,000,000 transitions and a `PlanError` ("no approximate level available ...") above that. `decide` runs the pilot and measures the rates unless they are injected: `r_kernel` is a repeated cached kernel call per outcome in the pilot, `r_solve` a short `solve` on a closed prefix of the pilot's own graph (not measured when the query fits even at 5e-5 s per edge, seven times R05's rate).
- `--exact` solves exactly however long it takes. An approximate answer has no `--route` or route page yet; its `--verify` is described under ticket #128 below.
- The marking lives in `cli/_marking.py` (text and JSON `answer` object: `approximate`, `lower`, `upper`, `gap`, `level`, `blocks`, `tolerance`, `within_tolerance`, `message`). The bounds printed are totals with the start item's price; the gap is that of the cost still to pay, the larger one.

### As built (ticket #128)

- The lift lives in `cli/_lift.py`: `lift(plan, steps)` is a `simulator.Policy` that takes a concrete item, compresses it, looks the exact state up in the policy table first (a level may keep some states exact) and else maps it to its block with `plan.approximation.coarsening.coarsen` and looks the block up; a key with no step stops the run. It is sound because the actions on the ladder are block-invariant: the pessimistic policy's action for a block is legal on every member of it. It reads the coarsening of the approximation and nothing about the level, so a level added later needs no change. An exact plan goes through the same function with the plain `compress` lookup.
- `--verify` of an approximate answer runs that policy with a fixed seed (1 by default) through the same `Memoized` actions. The verdict: it agrees when no run was unfinished or stopped and the simulated mean, within 5 standard errors on each side, meets `[lower, upper]` (totals, the start item's price included). The width is the 5 standard errors of the exact check, not the 95% interval that is printed: with a gap near 0 a 95% interval would reject a correct policy in one seed of twenty. The reason says whether the simulated mean lies below or above the certified interval. The detectable error is read against the middle of the bounds.
- `simulator.SimulationReport.cvar(level)` is the new estimator (the mean of the worst `1 - level` share of the run costs, by the Rockafellar-Uryasev form `q + E[(X - q)+] / (1 - level)`, its interval a normal approximation of the influence function). `--verify` uses level 0.9, the default of `risk.evaluate`.
- The text and the JSON of the verification are in `cli/_marking.py`. The text heads the block "Verification by simulation, not certified", and every line that shows a p90 or a CVaR number says "simulated" and "not certified". The JSON `verification` block has `label: "simulated, not certified"`, `simulated_mean_cost`, `simulated_p90`, `simulated_cvar` (with `level`), `certified_interval` and the verdict, and no `computed_p90`. A simulated interval is still never put in `risk.Estimate`.
- What the check can and cannot see: the lifted policy's true mean is its value in the pessimistic model, i.e. at the upper bound, so the simulated mean sits near `upper`. The check therefore catches an `upper` that is too low and a `lower` above the policy's cost, not a `lower` that is merely too high or an `upper` that is merely loose; the certificate (ADR-0010 and the proof above) covers those, the simulation only guards the lift and the kernels.
- Timings of a `--verify` run go in the PR and are never asserted (ADR-0014).

### As built (ticket #172)

- **Declarations.** `Reads.variants` says that an adding action's kernel depends on the member of a block only through the pool weight its pooled families hold. Augmentation, Regal and Exalted declare `Reads.adding(variants=True)`; Annulment declares `Reads(junk=True, families=frozenset(), block_invariant=True)` (it removes a uniform slot, so its exact state keeps family types instead of every family); Transmutation, Alteration and Scouring, erasing rerolls like the others, now declare `block_invariant`. The contract suite compares the members of blocks of the fixtures for all of them (for a `variants` action it checks that every member's backup lies between the smallest and the largest backup over the variants, for random continuation values, to 1e-12).
- **Pools of kinds.** A pool is a union of kinds: told-apart families (including the families of the influence mods of the start item, which the coarse state now gets from `start.flags.influences`) and whole family types `~n`. `Coarsening.variants(block, action, bound)` lists the distinct pushed kernels of the members that put each pool's slots on its lightest or heaviest free families (crafted slots first on the families that have a crafted mod); `kernel` keeps its meaning for a block-invariant action. `Coarsening.pooling_by_spread(abstraction, spread, start)` sorts the units of a side by per-family pool weight and starts a pool at the lightest unused unit, taking the units within `spread` of it; a pool of one unit is not made.
- **Min-max solve.** `solver.VariantAction` is an action whose outcomes are one of several variants. `solve_variants` is the pessimistic solve: nature's variant per (state, action) is the outer loop (Hoffman-Karp, at most 100 rounds), the controller's solve inside is the existing `solve`; the certificate is the residual of the final policy's worst variant on the states it reaches, with `g` the smallest cost of that policy's actions, as in "When the members differ: variants". `solve_bounds` solves the optimistic side as the plain model in which every variant is an action of its own (a min over both), and the pessimistic side with `solve_variants`; a model with no variant action gives exactly the numbers it gave before (a test compares them bit for bit). `tests/test_solver_minmax.py` is oracle (A): random games against brute force over every stationary policy and every choice of variant, bounds bracketing the exact value and agreeing to 1e-9. `tests/test_abstraction_variants_real.py` is oracle (C) on the real bow (skips without the cache).
- **The ladder.** A query with an addition has `SPREAD_LADDER` in place of the family-group rungs: spread 1000, then 500 (weight units of the pool; measured on a pool of 34 families of about 59,000 per side, not scaled to other bases), followed by the tail cuts on the finest rung, as for the family groups. A tail cut over a variants level uses the exact actions where it expands and the block bounds as leaves, so its argument needs only that the block bounds are a sub- and a supersolution of the exact backup (they are, by the extremes claim); the policy it shows has each variant action replaced by its dearest variant (`shown_policy`).
- **The pilot.** Additions and Annulment have no closed form: the pilot count walks them with real kernels (`needs_walk`), at most `ADDING_LIMIT` (20,000) states and a quarter of the budget in seconds (at least 2 s); a walk that stops is `complete=False`, and `decide` then tries the levels without a forecast (they are built to find their size). On the bow the walk stops after 5 s with 20,008 states. The levels of a `climb` may take ten budgets of seconds together (`LEVEL_ALLOWANCE`); when the time is spent the narrowest interval so far is returned with a message, or the query ends with "no coarse level finished" if none finished.
- **Two defects the first measurement found.** `actions.pool.pool_candidates` weighed every mod of the game for every pool (about 90 ms); the mods of a base, level and influence that can spawn are now cached (`_admitted`), so a pool costs about 2 ms. And the coarse state built its pool without the influence of the start item, so the Hunter families stayed exact (15,691 blocks at spread 3000, 416 s on the PC); `Coarsening` now takes the influences of the start state, and (since #69) the kinds of an influenced item are the family types of its own pool.
- **Routes with a lock or a tag metamod have no weight-spread level.** When the metamods are modelled (the start holds one, or an enabled action adds one) the kind of a slot carries the Attack/Caster tags of its mod, so the mods of one family can have different kinds, which a pool of kinds does not carry. `Coarsening` refuses an addition then (`OutOfScopeError`, "there is no weight-spread level for a route with a lock or a tag metamod"), and the CLI says "no approximate level available". A ticket must give the pools the tags.

## Risk numbers of an approximate answer

An approximate answer prints no p90 and no CVaR by default, and says so: the chain on blocks is not Markov, so `risk` cannot give its certified `Estimate`s, and the exact chain of the lifted policy is not built. With `--verify` the lifted policy is run on concrete items (ADR-0019, layer 4) and the report adds the simulated mean, p90 and CVaR with confidence intervals, in a separate type and labelled "simulated, not certified"; a simulated interval is never put in `risk.Estimate`. (A later option, not decided: the exact chain of the policy over only the states it visits, 2,609 of 13,172 on R05, through `risk` once #82 has made it fast.)

## With the recombinator interval (ADR-0012, ADR-0022)

The recombinator interval comes from a family of selection rules, one solve per rule. Recombinators are refused today, so no approximate answer has one. When one is supported, a per-rule 1% test would not certify the bracket (two rules with `V = 10` each, each rule's best policy costing 50 under the other: per-rule gaps 0%, bracket `[10, 50]`, 400%). The 1% claim applies to the bracket itself: the reported lower bound is `max_r V-_r`, the upper bound `min over candidate policies pi of max_r' J+_r'(pi)` (each candidate evaluated in the pessimistic model of rule `r'`), and the test is `upper / lower - 1 <= 1%`, solver errors included. The route interval of ADR-0012 for the candidate shown is `[min_r' J-_r'(pi), max_r' J+_r'(pi)]` with `J-` the policy's value in the optimistic model, labelled approximate.

## Not decided here

- R04 and the Fracturing Orb: the Fracturing Orb declares neither block invariance nor variants, so R04 has no approximate level (measured in #172, above); a ticket must prove one of the two for it. A route that models a lock or a tag metamod (R14 proper) has none either: the pools of kinds do not carry the Attack/Caster tags that the kinds then have (#172, above).
- Exact pruning by proof ([#118](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/118)): an action that only adds a mod nobody wants is dominated in B1 (the measurement is evidence, not a proof), but with Annulment such a mod dilutes the removal, so the proof is per action and belongs there. This ADR covers what is left when no proof exists.
- The memory of the solver (settled in [#126](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/126)): `solve` held an `Action` per action and an `Outcome` per transition (about 250 bytes per transition); it now keeps flat arrays (about 30 bytes per transition stored, about 55 at the peak of a solve) and rebuilds only the policy's actions, and `solve_bounds` runs it twice. The table of time and peak memory is in that pull request.
