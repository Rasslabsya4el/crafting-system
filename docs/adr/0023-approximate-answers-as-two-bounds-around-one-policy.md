# An approximate answer is an executable policy between two certified bounds

When the exact compressed state is too large to solve in about a minute, the planner solves two smaller models of a **coarse state** instead of one big one, and reports a policy that it can run on a real item together with an interval that provably holds the exact mean cost. The answer is marked **approximate** and its error is the certified gap, never a guess. This is the "approximate merges and their error measurement" of the map; the owner's tolerance is [#81](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/81) Q2: exact when a benchmark query fits in about a minute, past that an approximate answer whose measured error is at most 1% of the mean cost. It amends ADR-0009 ("v1 allows only exact merges"): an approximate merge is allowed, but only in this form.

## The two models

A **coarse state** is a partition of the exact compressed states into blocks (ADR-0009's states with some slot kinds renamed to a coarser kind). Two models are solved on the blocks:

- the **optimistic model**: a block offers every action that is legal in at least one of its members, and the cheapest member decides what it costs. Its value `V-` of a block is at most the exact optimum of every member (the exact optimum, restricted to a block, is a supersolution of this operator, and value iteration from zero rises to its least fixed point);
- the **pessimistic model**: a block offers an action only if it is legal in every member, and pays what the worst member pays. Its best policy, read on the members, is an ordinary policy of the exact problem, and its value `V+` is at least the exact cost of that policy (the policy's cost is the least fixed point of an operator that `V+` dominates).

So for every member `s` of a block `B`: `V-(B) <= V*(s) <= J(pi)(s) <= V+(B)`, where `pi` is the pessimistic policy lifted to the members. The answer shows `pi` and the mean `V+` (the cost it can really promise), and the **certified gap** `V+/V- - 1`, which is at least the true error of `pi`. It meets the 1% tolerance when the gap is at most 1%. This is a bounded-parameter (abstraction) MDP bound, and it needs no concrete simulator to hold; the simulator (ADR-0019, layer 4) still runs the lifted policy on concrete items as an independent check, and the risk numbers of an approximate answer (p90, CVaR) come from that run, with a confidence interval, because the chain on blocks is not Markov.

## Which merges are allowed

An approximate merge is allowed when both models can be written down; each costs error in a way that is measured below.

1. **Family groups: pool the families no requirement cares about.** A junk family is told apart in ADR-0009 only because an enabled bench craft would clash with it. Families whose mods give no requirement and that no goal-relevant craft clashes with are pooled into groups; relevant ones stay apart, and status, counts and sides are never merged. A reroll (Chaos Orb, Harvest reforge, essence) erases the junk and redraws it, so its kernel on a block is the exact kernel relabelled: no error. Only the bench is approximate: a craft whose family `phi` lies in a group of `k` families, with `n` junk or crafted slots of that group on the item, is legal in the optimistic model when `n <= k - 1` and in the pessimistic model only when `n = 0`. A crafted mod with no requirement is pooled in the same way. The error is zero when the pooled crafts are never part of the best policy (the case of R05), and otherwise the gap of the two models says how much the pooling can cost.
2. **Tail cut: expand exactly only where the policy goes.** Following a coarse policy, states with an expected number of visits below `theta` are not expanded: a cut successor takes the optimistic and pessimistic value of its block as its two leaf values. The result is again a lower and an upper bound, and it is as tight as the leaf bounds are. With a leaf that knows nothing (a lower bound of the cheapest action) the lower bound is useless: the cut buys states only on top of a coarse model.
3. **Weight buckets: not now.** Rounding pool weights to a geometric grid (needed if Annulment and Exalted make family types by weight matter) does give a bound, by an interval kernel (every outcome probability may be off by a factor `r^4` for four draws, nature picks the best or the worst distribution inside the interval at each backup), but it is loose: see the table. It is deferred until a benchmark route needs it.

Not allowed: any merge that changes what an action can do or cost on the goal-relevant part of the state, or that is not backed by both models. A merge that loses a requirement's status, a slot count or a side is never an approximate merge, it is a wrong one.

## Measurement

Prototype in `.claude/scratch/83-approx/` of the main checkout (not committed; `src/` is untouched). Setup: the R05 ring of [#59](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/59) (`Metadata/Items/Rings/Ring1`, item level 86, real 3.29.3.3 data, fractured All Attributes, goal Life and the forced Strength, a Deafening Essence of Rage, Remove Crafted Mods and all 141 ring bench crafts). Prices are placeholders (Essence 2 Chaos, each craft the Chaos value of its bench cost in a made-up currency table); only ratios count. The exact graph was built by the real `Abstraction`: 13,172 states, 22,994,524 transitions, 906 s (674 s in kernels). Policies are lifted to the exact graph and their exact cost computed there. The solver of the prototype is numpy value iteration; it equals `solve` to nine digits on the 76-state graph.

"Direct build" is a representative of each block run through the real kernels with the outcomes mapped to blocks (the cost of a builder that never expands the 13,172 states); it ran while another job shared the machine, so it is an upper bound. Reading:

- Only M1 fits the minute (38 s plus 1.3 s of solving, 365 blocks, 36 times fewer than exact); the build time is 0.1 s per block, almost all of it turning the 1,800 outcomes of an essence into states, which a builder working on the cached label distribution can skip.
- Every level has a zero gap on R05. That is not luck: the 109 crafts that give neither Life nor Strength are never in the best policy (the exact optimum with 32 crafts is the same, 6.871663 at flat prices, and 2.159763 at the made-up prices), and a craft that matters is blocked only by a family that the state keeps apart through its contribution. A level that cannot hurt this route does not show what a harmful merge costs; the gap is what would show it, and it stays zero where the best policy does not use the merged information.
- The policy of M1 visits 2,609 of the 13,172 states (1.95 million of 23.0 million transitions). Evaluating it exactly on the concrete model, lazily, would cost about 8% of the exact build (about a minute): an optional tightening of the upper bound, not needed when the gap is small.
- **Tail cut** with M1's bounds as leaves: zero gap at every `theta` (28 states kept at 1e-2 up to 2,577 at 1e-6), since the leaves are exact. With a leaf of no information the upper bound converges fast (2.485 at 1,218 kept states, 2.173 at 2,243 against 2.160), the lower bound does not (0.02): the leaf lower bound must come from a coarse model.
- **Weight buckets** on the 76-state graph (rounding to ratio `r`): the model's value moves by -0.03% (r = 1.05), +0.04% (1.25), -0.09% (1.5), -0.02% (2), -0.48% (4) and the policy's exact cost does not move at all, but the certified gap is 1.2% at r = 1.05, 4.2% at 1.25, 6.4% at 1.5, 7.5% at 2 and 8.0% at 4. A bound the planner can certify needs buckets of about 4% or finer, which merge few weights (337 distinct weights fall to 152 at 5%).

## How the CLI decides, and how it marks the result

Before solving, the CLI runs a **pilot count**: one cached kernel of the first random action (1 s for the essence of R05, 2,061 outcomes), then the number of crafted slots that fit each outcome by sets of families and free slots, with no further kernel call. On R05 it gives 76 states for 32 crafts and 13,172 for 141, both equal to the exact counts, in 0.3 s and 1.1 s. It predicts 27.1 million transitions (23.0 million built; 18% over) and, at the pilot's measured 38 microseconds per outcome, 1,018 s of kernel time (674 s built; the pilot ran while the machine was loaded). The rule: exact if the predicted kernel time plus the solve fits about a minute, otherwise the **ladder**: M1, then smaller groups of the irrelevant families (and then a tail cut with M1's bounds as leaves), stopping at the first level whose certified gap is at most 1%. A level is chosen from its own pilot count the same way. The #59 formula (rarity times crafts times junk configurations) overestimates this route by two orders of magnitude (1.2 million states against 13,172) and is kept only as a cheap upper bound for the targets whose structure the pilot cannot count yet.

The result carries the words "approximate (certified within X% of the exact mean)", the two bounds, the number of blocks and the level; in the JSON `approximate: true`, `lower`, `upper`, `gap`, `level`. When no level reaches 1% inside the time, the planner still prints the interval, says that the gap is above 1%, and offers the exact run with its predicted time or a smaller craft list; it never rounds a gap down. `--verify` runs the lifted policy in the simulator and reports the mean with its interval next to the bounds.

## Not decided here

- Annulment and Exalted (map [#67](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/67)): the junk's pool weight then matters, and pooled families need an interval on the pool total (the weight-bucket machinery above in a milder form); R04, R13 and R14 (10^6 to 10^7 states by the #59 estimate) are not measured here, only R05 is. The build ticket for the family groups must re-measure on the first of them.
- Exact pruning by proof ([#118](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/118)): an action that only adds a mod nobody wants is dominated in B1 (the measurement above is evidence, not a proof), but with Annulment such a mod dilutes the removal, so the proof is per action and belongs there. This ADR covers what is left when no proof exists.
- The memory of the solver: the first attempt to solve the 13,172 states with the repo's `solve` (all `Action` objects of 23 million transitions in memory) ended without a result or a traceback (7 GB were in use at the last look, 3 GB of the machine's memory were free). Its time on this graph is therefore not reported. [#82](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/82) and the build ticket for the bounds must keep the model's memory per transition small.
