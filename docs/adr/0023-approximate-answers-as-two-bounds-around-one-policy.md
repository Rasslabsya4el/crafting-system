# An approximate answer is an executable policy between two certified bounds

When the exact compressed state is too large to solve in about a minute, the planner solves two smaller models of a **coarse state** instead of one big one, and reports a policy that it can run on a real item together with an interval that provably holds the exact mean cost. The answer is marked **approximate** and its error is the certified gap, never a guess. This is the "approximate merges and their error measurement" of the map; the owner's tolerance is [#81](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/81) Q2: exact when a benchmark query fits in about a minute, past that an approximate answer whose measured error is at most 1% of the mean cost. It amends ADR-0009 ("v1 allows only exact merges"): an approximate merge is allowed, but only in this form.

## The two models and the gap

A **coarse state** is a partition of the exact compressed states into blocks (ADR-0009's states with some slot kinds renamed to a coarser kind). Two models are solved on the blocks, each taken **per member**: for every member of a block and every action, the member's exact kernel is pushed through the block map.

- the **optimistic model** offers, in a block, every action that is legal in at least one member and charges the cheapest member. Its value `V-` of a block is at most the exact optimum `V*` of every member (the restriction of `V*` to a block is a supersolution of this operator, and value iteration from zero rises to its least fixed point);
- the **pessimistic model** offers an action only if it is legal in every member and charges the worst member. Its best policy `pi`, read on the members, is an ordinary policy of the exact problem, and its value `V+` is at least `pi`'s exact cost `J(pi)`.

So `V-(B) <= V*(s) <= J(pi)(s) <= V+(B)` for every member `s` of `B`. The answer shows `pi` and the mean `V+`, and the **certified gap** `V+/V- - 1`. Because `V*` lies in the interval too, the gap bounds the true error of the policy shown, `J(pi)/V* - 1 <= V+/V- - 1`, and bounds the error of the mean it reports the same way. It meets the tolerance when it is at most 1%. (Checked against brute force on 300 random models, 1,800 state checks, largest violation 4e-15, by the `checker` of PR #121.)

The solver's own error enters the gap. `solve` returns values with `V(1-e) <= V* <= V(1+e)` (`Solution.error_bound`, ADR-0010), so the reported lower bound is `V-(1-e-)` and the upper `V+(1+e+)`; the gap is `V+(1+e+) / (V-(1-e-)) - 1`. A table in this ADR is **certified** only when its numbers come from `solve` with this correction; the numbers from the prototype's numpy value iteration are called measured.

### Per-member, never from one representative

A block may be built from one representative member only for an action whose kernel, pushed through the block map, is the same for all members (**block-invariant**). Otherwise the bound is false. Example: a block `{s1, s2}`, `s1` reaches the goal at cost 1, `s2` loops with probability 0.9 (true cost 10); the representative `s1` gives an "upper" bound of 1. The actions that are block-invariant when only slots without a requirement are renamed:

- an erasing reroll: Chaos Orb, Harvest reforge, essences and **fossils in a resonator** (`ApplyFossils` is in `ERASING` of `abstraction/_kinds.py`, ADR-0009): the outcome depends on the flags, the fractured mods and the action, which a block keeps whole. This covers the 420-pool Tangled sets of [#118](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/118);
- a bench craft and Remove Crafted Mods: deterministic, the successor block is a function of the block and the craft; only legality differs between members.

For these the models differ only in which actions are offered, so both are ordinary models and need no change in the solver. The build proves block-invariance for each action class it supports (a test compares every member of every block on the fixtures, tolerance 1e-9; the prototype did it on all 13,172 members of R05 and all 386 of the harder route below, largest difference 0). **Every other action refuses the coarse state until a ticket proves its invariance.** The reasons differ: an addition (Exalted, Regal, Augmentation, Alteration, Transmutation, Alchemy) draws from a pool whose total weight depends on which families the item holds, so members whose pooled families have different weights give different kernels (pooling only families of equal weight is exact and is already ADR-0009's family type); Annulment chooses uniformly among the removable mods (`docs/research/2026-10-30-2-state-compression.md`), so a count per group probably keeps it invariant, but nothing has been built or tested yet, so it is refused until its invariance test exists ([#67](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/67)); the recombinator, imprint and Awakener's Orb of ADR-0012/0022 read the mods of two items and are unanalysed. A query that enables a refused action has no family-group level: it is exact or ends with "no approximate level available". The approximate level therefore covers only queries made of erasing rerolls, bench crafts and Remove Crafted Mods.

### If a later action needs the members to differ (variants)

A model whose successor distribution depends on the member would make the pessimistic backup a max over members (variants), i.e. a min-max game. ADR-0010's certificate survives it. It uses only `T_mu(aV) = a T_mu V + (1-a) k_mu` with `k_mu` the constant term of the policy (cost plus the fixed downstream part) and a lower bound `g` on all `k`. For the operator `T+_mu V = max_v (c + P_v V + out_v)`, each variant satisfies the identity with its own `k_v`, so for `a >= 1` `T+_mu(aV) <= a T+_mu V - (a-1) g` and for `a <= 1` `T+_mu(aV) >= a T+_mu V + (1-a) g` with `g` the smallest constant term over all actions and variants; the multipliers `g/(g-e)` and `g/(g+d)` and the supersolution argument are unchanged. It is not built in v1 (no action needs it); the build ticket that adds one must supply an oracle (brute force over policies and variants).

## Which merges are allowed

1. **Family groups: pool the families no requirement cares about.** A junk family is told apart in ADR-0009 only because an enabled bench craft would clash with it. Families whose mods give no requirement, and that no goal-relevant craft clashes with, are pooled into groups; relevant ones stay apart, and status, counts and sides are never merged. A junk mod with several families (`held_families`) is never pooled: its kind stays its family key, as does any crafted mod with a requirement. Fractured mods, told-apart junk and told-apart crafted mods hold their families exactly; let `H` be the families of a group `g` of `k` families that they hold. A craft of family `phi` in `g` is illegal in both models if `phi` is in `H` (the fractured mod's family included, as in R05's fractured All Attributes); otherwise, with `n` pooled slots of `g` on the item, it is legal in the optimistic model when `n <= k - |H| - 1` and in the pessimistic model only when `n = 0`. A crafted mod with no requirement is pooled in the same way. Only for the block-invariant actions above.
2. **Tail cut: expand exactly only where the policy goes.** Following a coarse policy, states with an expected number of visits below `theta` are not expanded; a cut successor takes the optimistic and the pessimistic value of its block as its two leaf values. As tight as the leaf bounds, so it needs a coarse model to start from.
3. **Weight buckets: not now.** Rounding pool weights to a geometric grid gives a bound by an interval kernel (nature picks the best or worst distribution inside `[p / r^4, p r^4]` at each backup, the variants argument above), but it is loose: 1.2% already at 5% buckets.

Not allowed: any merge that changes what an action can do or cost on the goal-relevant part of the state, or that lacks both models.

## How the coarse state is built (ticket #125)

`abstraction.Coarsening(abstraction, groups)` is the family-group level. A **block** is a `CompressedState` whose pooled slots have the kind `=g<i>`; `coarsen(state)` is the map and `kernel(block, action, bound)` the kernel of an action in the optimistic (`"lower"`) or pessimistic (`"upper"`) model, taken per member and pushed through `coarsen`.

- **What is pooled.** Only families that an enabled action tells apart (the families of the crafts it reads), one name per family, that no requirement names (an ordinary mod of the family in a leaf: refused with `ValueError`). `pooling_irrelevant(abstraction, size)` also leaves out the families a craft that gives a requirement clashes with (the blockers of the goal craft) and cuts the rest, sorted by name, into groups of `size`. A group may list families of both sides; its blocks pool them per side, so `k` is the number of the group's families on the side of the slot. The groups of 8 and 4 of the measurement below are cut this way (the same block counts: 365, 1,582, 4,295 on R05).
- **What stays exact.** A junk mod with several families (kind = its family key), a hybrid crafted mod, fractured mods, a crafted mod whose family is not pooled, and slots whose kind is already `""`. They hold their families in every member: `H` of the rule above is read from them.
- **Legality is read from the action, not from a rule of the bench.** The members of a block are the ways to put its `n` pooled slots on distinct families of the pool outside `H` (a pooled crafted slot on a family that has a crafted mod). For an action the builder runs the exact kernel on one representative member for each way the families in `Reads.families` can be held, and the other families of the pool stand for each other; the lower model offers the action if one representative can take it, the upper only if all can. For a bench craft that is the rule above (`n <= k - |H| - 1` and `n = 0`, illegal in both when its family is in `H`), and a Remove Crafted Mods or an erasing reroll (no family read) has one representative. A new method declares `Reads(families=...)` and `block_invariant`; nothing in `abstraction` names it. An enabled action that is not declared block-invariant, reads every family, or adds mods and keeps the junk (`Reads.adding()`, `pool_weight`: its kernel depends on the pool weight of the families held, and pooling families of different weights changes it) makes the constructor raise `OutOfScopeError`; an action asked for a kernel that is not declared raises it too. A limit, not a decision: pooling only families of equal weight is exact for an adding action and is already the family type of ADR-0009, so a later ticket may lift it.
- **A gap needs a family that can be held instead.** Pooling the two blockers of one hybrid craft with nothing else loses nothing (every member holds one of them, so the craft is blocked in all); pooling a blocker with another family does (a member holds the other, so the craft is open in one model only). The committed fixture shows both.

**Other kinds** (prototype, R05 and its 76-state version): tail cut with M1's bounds as leaves, zero gap at every `theta` (28 states kept at 1e-2, 2,577 at 1e-6, since the leaves are exact); with a leaf that knows nothing the upper bound converges (2.485 at 1,218 kept states, 2.173 at 2,243, against 2.160) and the lower bound does not (0.02), so the leaf lower bound must come from a coarse model. What could reach 1% for them is open: variants with an interval on the pool total of the pooled families (the certificate argument above, plus an oracle), the tail cut on top of such a model, exact pruning by proof ([#118](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/118)) and a smaller craft list chosen by the user. Until one of them is built and measured, such a query is exact or says that no approximate level is available.

## How the CLI decides, and how it marks the result

Before solving, the CLI runs a **pilot count**: one cached kernel of the first random action (1 s for the essence of R05, 2,061 outcomes), then the crafted slots that fit each outcome by sets of families and free slots, with no further kernel call. On R05 it gives 76 states for 32 crafts and 13,172 for 141, equal to the exact counts, in 0.3 s and 1.1 s, and predicts 27.1 million transitions (23.0 million built, 18% over). The #59 formula (rarity times crafts times junk configurations) overestimates this route by two orders of magnitude (1.2 million states) and stays only a cheap upper bound for targets the pilot cannot count yet.

The predicted time is `transitions * (r_kernel + r_solve)`: `r_kernel` is measured in the pilot (the cost per outcome of a second, cached kernel call: 38 microseconds on a loaded machine; the build took 906 s / 23.0 million = 39 microseconds per transition including everything), `r_solve` is the seconds per edge of `solve`, measured by a short calibration solve on the pilot's own first layers (about 7 microseconds in the M3 run). Both rates are parameters of the decision function, so that a test injects them and no test depends on a machine (ADR-0014). For R05 with 141 crafts the prediction is about 1,200 s against 906 s built plus an estimated 160 s of solving. The pilot's "within 18%" and the solve rate come from one route (R05) and from models of up to 2.1 million edges; they are not a general guarantee.

The rule: exact if the predicted time fits about a minute, otherwise the **ladder**: M1, then smaller groups of the irrelevant families, then a tail cut with M1's bounds as leaves (a tail cut needs a coarse model for its leaves, so it only follows a coarse level), stopping at the first level whose certified gap is at most 1%; a level is built only if its own pilot count fits. When a refused action leaves no coarse level, the query is exact or ends with "no approximate level available". When no level reaches 1% inside the time, the planner prints the best interval it has and says that the gap is above 1%; it never rounds a gap down. It offers the exact run only when the predicted transitions are at most 2 million, the largest size the product `solve` has been run on here (30 s, memory not measured); beyond that it says that the exact run is not offered until ticket B has measured memory at larger sizes, and offers a smaller craft list.

The result carries the words "approximate (certified within X% of the exact mean)", the two bounds, the block count and the level; in the JSON `approximate: true`, `lower`, `upper`, `gap`, `level`.

### As built (ticket #127)

- `Abstraction.estimate(starts, actions)` is the pilot count. It walks the graph the way `solve` does (a goal is not expanded) but runs the real kernel once per (reroll, key), the key being the flags, the fractured mods and, for a reroll that erases crafted mods, only which metamods are held (a crafted metamod makes a reroll refuse), and decides bench crafts and Remove Crafted Mods itself by sets of families and free slots. A property test compares its states and transitions with the real expansion on random craft subsets and starts. An action of another shape (an addition, say) raises `EstimateRefused` and the query is built exactly, without a forecast. `Estimate.coarsened(coarsening)` forecasts a coarse level from the same states (the blocks are the images of the reached states; a forecast, not a bound).
- `cli._decide.choose(estimate, r_kernel, r_solve, budget, levels=, refusal=)` reads no clock. Predicted time is kernel outcomes times `r_kernel` plus solved edges times `r_solve` (for a coarse level two models are solved). Exact when it fits `--budget` (60 s by default); otherwise the levels of `LADDER` whose own forecast fits; with a `refusal` (an offered action that does not declare `Reads.block_invariant`) or no level that fits, the query is exact if it is at most 2,000,000 transitions and a `PlanError` ("no approximate level available ...") above that. `decide` runs the pilot and measures the rates unless they are injected: `r_kernel` is a repeated cached kernel call per outcome in the pilot, `r_solve` a short `solve` on a closed prefix of the pilot's own graph (not measured when the query fits even at 5e-5 s per edge, seven times R05's rate).
- `--exact` solves exactly however long it takes. An approximate answer has no `--verify`, `--route` or route page yet.
- The marking lives in `cli/_marking.py` (text and JSON `answer` object: `approximate`, `lower`, `upper`, `gap`, `level`, `blocks`, `tolerance`, `within_tolerance`, `message`). The bounds printed are totals with the start item's price; the gap is that of the cost still to pay, the larger one.

## Risk numbers of an approximate answer

An approximate answer prints no p90 and no CVaR by default, and says so: the chain on blocks is not Markov, so `risk` cannot give its certified `Estimate`s, and the exact chain of the lifted policy is not built. With `--verify` the lifted policy is run on concrete items (ADR-0019, layer 4) and the report adds the simulated mean, p90 and CVaR with confidence intervals, in a separate type and labelled "simulated, not certified"; a simulated interval is never put in `risk.Estimate`. (A later option, not decided: the exact chain of the policy over only the states it visits, 2,609 of 13,172 on R05, through `risk` once #82 has made it fast.)

## With the recombinator interval (ADR-0012, ADR-0022)

The recombinator interval comes from a family of selection rules, one solve per rule. Recombinators are refused today, so no approximate answer has one. When one is supported, a per-rule 1% test would not certify the bracket (two rules with `V = 10` each, each rule's best policy costing 50 under the other: per-rule gaps 0%, bracket `[10, 50]`, 400%). The 1% claim applies to the bracket itself: the reported lower bound is `max_r V-_r`, the upper bound `min over candidate policies pi of max_r' J+_r'(pi)` (each candidate evaluated in the pessimistic model of rule `r'`), and the test is `upper / lower - 1 <= 1%`, solver errors included. The route interval of ADR-0012 for the candidate shown is `[min_r' J-_r'(pi), max_r' J+_r'(pi)]` with `J-` the policy's value in the optimistic model, labelled approximate.

## Not decided here

- Non-erasing actions (map [#67](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/67)) and R04, R13, R14: see "No merge in this ADR reaches 1%". The first build ticket must re-measure on the first of them.
- Exact pruning by proof ([#118](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/118)): an action that only adds a mod nobody wants is dominated in B1 (the measurement is evidence, not a proof), but with Annulment such a mod dilutes the removal, so the proof is per action and belongs there. This ADR covers what is left when no proof exists.
- The memory of the solver (settled in [#126](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/126)): `solve` held an `Action` per action and an `Outcome` per transition (about 250 bytes per transition); it now keeps flat arrays (about 30 bytes per transition stored, about 55 at the peak of a solve) and rebuilds only the policy's actions, and `solve_bounds` runs it twice. The table of time and peak memory is in that pull request.
