# A donor's want is the projection its consumer reads; Needs are offered by a lower-bound loop

ADR-0022 prices a second item by the value of an acquisition process aimed at a `want`, and leaves open what a `want` is, which `Need`s an action offers from the target, and how many. [Ticket #211](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/211) built a throwaway prototype on the real `solver` (`Acquisition`, `solve`) and measured it: a recombinator world sized like R25 and an Awakener world sized like R18, each against the product with every donor offered up front, against exact brute force on a tiny case, and against the Bellman linear programme (the prototype is `research/211/` in commit e66131c of the branch of the pull request; it is not in the tree; `tests/test_donor_wants_reference.py` keeps its fixtures). The decisions come first, the numbers after.

## Decisions

1. **A want is a class, not a state: the projection of the donor that its consumer reads.** `Need.want` stands for `(kind, key)`, where `key` is what the consuming action reads of the donor, and the acquisition ends at the first item whose key equals it (`Acquisition(reaches=...)`, which exists). The action declares the projection; the abstraction computes it. For the **Awakener's Orb** (ADR-0032) the key is the donor's influence and its influenced mods (by name for the mods the target names, by class for the others): the rest of the donor is rerolled and never read. For the **recombinator** (ADR-0033) the key is the whole compressed item: the target mods by id, the other mods by side and weight class, and the fields copied from the base (influences, implicits, quality, Memory Strands), because the output depends on all of them. A want is a record of fields, so the strand count `s` of B5-4 ([#277](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/277), [#290](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/290)) is one more field that joins a projection only where an action reads it (a recombinator reads the base's strands, the Awakener's Orb does not); nothing about the shape blocks it.
2. **The projection is the exact class, and wider than the item.** Asking for one whole item (the donor with the influenced mod and nothing else) is an over-restriction: it makes the acquisition reach one member of the class, and a member it passed on the way is thrown away. The projection costs less and loses nothing, because the kernel is the same for every member. Measured below on two R18-shaped worlds (c1, c2): whole-item wants raise the value by 0.7% and 20% and multiply the product by 3.8 and 7.7 (c1 counted on the development PC, c2 on a runner).
3. **Junk is kept by weight class in a donor's want, and the kernel averages the overlap.** Two inputs share a junk mod by chance (a mod on both inputs enters the pool twice, ADR-0033). In class mode the kernel of `(receiver, donor)` averages the number of mods the two share over the hypergeometric law of names in each class. This is exact for any policy that does not look at junk names, and it is the value of the planner (which cannot tell them apart); a crafter who steers a donor's junk names towards the receiver's could do better. It did not matter in any case measured (below), and T7 carries the claim.
4. **`Need`s are offered by a loop that proves the rest unneeded** (ADR-0031 style, with the value in hand as the bound). Solve with the donors offered so far; then for every state and every donor not offered check `cost + L(donor) + sum p(j) L(j) >= V(state)`, where `L` is the value in hand on the states that were solved and the **determinized distance** (the cheapest path to a state of the want, any possible successor, inputs free) elsewhere; offer each donor that fails; solve again; stop when none fails. The result is the value of the full model, with the donors left out proved unneeded. The proof is in the next section. The loop starts from a seed (the donors made only of the target's mods, "isolated feeders", for a recombinator; the donor the route names for the Awakener's Orb); any seed ends at the same value, a seed of nothing costs four times the wants.
5. **Offers are per base state**, shared by every acquisition process, not per product state. Per product state looked cheaper on paper (an acquisition process offers only what it needs) and was worse for the recombinator in every size measured (acquisition processes, the main target included: 38 against 8 at class size, 259 against 8 at raw size, 31 against 8 at a pool of eight, 14 against 8 at six), because the weak lower bound of a process not yet built asks for more of them; for the Awakener it made no difference (2 against 2 in c2).
6. **The bound must be the expected value, not the best case.** Replacing the sum over the outcomes by the best outcome keeps 3,389 of 3,906 (state, donor) pairs and all 63 processes in the class case (table below); only the expected value prunes.
7. **The branching component of an unrestricted donor, and of the lazy one, is beyond `direct_solve_limit` (64) and needs #179's sparse policy iteration, which exists.** Offering every donor makes the whole product one component (3,844 of 3,969 states at class size, 8,464 of 8,649 at a raw size of eight mods); the lazy product with its seed offered everywhere is still one component of 434 (class) and 2,086 (raw) states, because the donors it keeps feed each other (AB needs A, A needs B). The Awakener's lazy product has none. `direct_solve_limit` stays 64, and `solve`, its `Model` and its sparse path are used as they are; the loop is new code beside them.
8. **The loop is compatible with an envelope search** ([#312](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/312)): see "The envelope search" below. A build that takes `Need`s through a per-state generator keeps both.
9. **Readings share their offers.** The interval driver (ADR-0022, T10) evaluates the policy of one reading under every other, so a donor offered in any reading of an axis is offered in all; a superset of offers leaves each model exact.

## Why the loop is exact

Let `M` be the model with every candidate donor offered, `T` its Bellman operator, `V*` the least fixed point (ADR-0022), and `V_k` the value of the model `M_k` with the donors offered so far (`V_k >= V*` because a policy of `M_k` is a policy of `M`). Let `L` be `V_k` on the states of `M_k` and the determinized distance `D` elsewhere. If `L <= T L` everywhere then `L <= V*` (where `V*` is infinite there is nothing to prove): `T` is monotone, so `L <= T^n L`, and `T^n L <= T_mu^n L` for the optimal policy `mu` (whose matrix has spectral radius below one), which tends to `V*`. The inequality holds at the states of `M_k` for the actions of `M_k` (`V_k` is a fixed point of their operator) and for the others exactly when the test of decision 4 passes; it holds at the other states because `D(s) <= c + min_j D(j) <= c + sum p_j L(j) + sum_y L(y)`, since `L >= D` and the inputs are not negative. So `V_k <= V* <= V_k`. A state of `M_k` with no finite value has every donor offered and its successors checked, so nothing is pruned where there is no incumbent. The values are exact to the certified `error_bound` of `solve` (1e-10 by default) and to the slack of the test, which the prototype and the fixtures take as a relative 1e-9 (a donor that would save less than that, about 4e-6 at the value 3,896, is not offered). `L` is the value in hand where that is finite; at a state whose value is infinite the code takes `D` (weaker, so still sound; `L = infinity` there would be sound too, since `T^n L <= T_mu^n L` only visits states the optimal policy reaches, and these are all finite when `V*` is).

## The envelope search

#312 moves the planner from whole-graph enumeration to an envelope search with bounds. The loop is that search in outline: `V_k` is the upper bound of the envelope, `D` the lower bound outside it, the test is the decision to expand a state's offers, and offering a donor expands a new acquisition process. Nothing in it needs the whole graph:

- The test needs, per state, the value in hand, a lower bound on the successors and the candidate list; `Abstraction.inputs(state, action)` already lists the candidates and the kernel gives the successors. The candidate list is generated when a state is expanded, not up front.
- It can be restricted to the states the policy in hand reaches. A state joins the checked set when an action offered at a checked state falls below the value there only because its successor was not checked. This gave the same value as checking everything in 40 of 40 random worlds (`test_testing_only_the_states_the_policy_reaches_gives_the_same_value`); it is the expansion rule of the search. It did not look at fewer states in total in these seven-state worlds (it needed more rounds), so its saving is for a search that does not solve the whole product each round.
- It stays exact only with **exact** cuts. A decision-relevance cut of #312 (outcomes below a probability lumped into one conservative outcome) makes `V_k` the value of a different model, and the loop then proves the cut model optimal, not the full one; the report must say which.
- Wants are keys that are made when a donor is offered; a want that nothing asks for costs nothing. Exact merging of states ([#312](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/312)) acts on an acquisition process like any other process: it is an ordinary process over compressed states, and the projection of decision 1 is itself an exact merge of donors.
- Any better lower bound plugs in at `D` (the loop stays exact if it is a sub-solution) and any better upper bound at `V_k`.

## What was measured

All values are expected costs in the prototype's prices (Alteration 1, Annulment 20, Exalted Orb 50, recombination 140), **not** the price file's, and the prototype is a world of its own: prefixes only for the recombinator (the count table of ADR-0033, doubling and family closing, `alpha = 1`; no base choice, item level or suffix side), an Awakener's Orb without the group conflict. State, action and transition counts do not depend on the machine; seconds are on GitHub's runners (`ubuntu-latest`, one process each), except where a line says it was counted on the development PC. An **acquisition process** is the main target or one want.

The raw value equals the class value to 12 digits at R25 size. The best-case bound and the seed of nothing were not run at raw size, and the product with every donor offered was not solved at raw size 8 or 12: it has 807,576 and 26.6 million recombination actions, which is the argument for the loop.

**Raw against class**, random worlds of three targets and two junk classes (weights, prices and target weights drawn at random, 14 worlds, each solved raw and class by the loop): the largest relative difference between the optimum with every mod named and the optimum of the class model is 1.8e-13 (rounding); in none does the optimal policy offer a donor with a junk mod. A policy that steers a donor's junk names towards the receiver's has not been found to beat the planner's.

In c1 (a smaller ring, counted on the development PC) the loop has 481 product states and the unrestricted one 3,855, the values are 79.91219068290502 and 79.91219068290364, and whole-item wants give 14,649 states and 80.4673 (+0.7%). **Agreement with brute force and the linear programme** (`tests/test_donor_wants_reference.py`): the tiny recombinator case (items of up to two prefixes from A and B, 12 product states, 19,683 policies) equals exact brute force over every stationary policy, 17.1616161616; the tiny Awakener case (6 base states, a donor as projection and as whole item) equals brute force for both, and the projection is never the dearer (2 against 5 by hand); 40 random worlds of up to three mods, with random prices and weights, give the same value for the lazy loop (both bounds, a seed of nothing and of the single mods, every state or the policy's envelope only), for the product with every donor offered, and, where every value is finite, for the Bellman linear programme of HiGHS; the lower bound the loop uses to leave a donor out is below the true cost of that action, pair by pair, and the determinized distance is below the value of every state of every acquisition, in the same worlds. The Awakener's lazy loop equals the unrestricted product in 14 of 14 random worlds of the prototype (counted on the development PC; tiny rings of two slots per side), and the whole-item product is never cheaper than the projection and was dearer in 1 of them.

## What this means for the build

- **T2b** ([#212](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/212)) builds the loop above in `solver` and `cli/_offers.py`: it takes `Abstraction.inputs` for the candidates, a seed from the target, the sparse path of #179 for the branching components, and the determinized distance as the first lower bound. `solve` stays unchanged; each round is a fresh solve (no warm start was needed: one or two rounds for the Awakener, two for the recombinator with a seed of isolated feeders, four to eight with a seed of nothing or per product state).
- **T4 ([#214](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/214)) and T7 ([#217](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/217))** own the projections: each defines, for its action, the function from a compressed donor to its key and declares it with the action's `Reads`, with a test that the kernel is equal for two donors with one key. The key of the Awakener's Orb is the influence and the influenced mods; the key of the recombinator is the compressed item with its base-copied fields and, from #277, the strand count.
- **The kernel of a class donor** averages the overlap of names (decision 3). T7's contract test compares it with the raw kernel on finished items, ADR-0019 layer 1.
- **The price of a bad seed** is rounds and wants, never an answer: an empty seed gave the same value with four times the wants.

## What is not decided here

The lower bound is the weakest that is still exact. Bellman sweeps started from the determinized distance, with every donor at its own determinized cost, changed nothing at pool size six (counted on the development PC: two and five sweeps gave the same 14 processes and 434 states as none), so the processes of a seed of nothing (34 donors of 62 at class size) are the measure of what a better bound could save, and #312 owns that. A route with a given donor list (the benchmark routes) is the best seed. The recombinator's predictable mode, the base choice, the suffix side and the exclusive mods are T5 and T7; they add fields to the key and states to the item, not a new kind of want. Whether the name-blind value of decision 3 can be beaten is not known; no world tried could.
