# The solver condenses SCCs, eliminates self-loops exactly, and starts in plain Python

The solver generates the reachable states, eliminates self-loops exactly, condenses strongly connected components and solves them in reverse topological order, with value or policy iteration inside a component and a Bellman-residual stopping bound. Risk is computed on the fixed policy (ADR-0006). Probabilities come from integer weights; the solver works in float64.

The code is Python first; numpy or numba only for hot loops a profile has found. Not in v1: FTVI-style bound pruning (unless fossil combinations make B1 too slow), numba before profiling, an LP solver in production. HiGHS LP and brute force are test oracles (ADR-0019). numpy is now a dependency, used by `risk` and, for one matrix inverse, by the route view's flow (`cli/_route_flow.py`: the inverse of the chain of steps, where a profile of ticket #96 found the pure-Python dense inverse taking 2.8 s at 300 steps; `numpy.linalg.inv` takes 0.5 s at 1000). For `risk` the profile of ticket #82 found the budget DP of `risk.evaluate` (one step per state and cost bin) spending its time in Python loops, and vectorising it gave about a tenfold gain. `solver` stays stdlib-only. The DP's work grows with states times the cost spread in bins, so for a large policy `evaluate` raises the grid step until the work fits a budget (default 3e8 entries); the intervals widen and stay certified, and `grid.step` reports it. The solver's dead-end search settles the strongly connected components sink first, so chains no longer cost a rescan per dead end.

The socket finish of ADR-0045 is the exception to the solver solving every sub-problem (ADR-0058): its Chromatic Orb rerolls up to 4,096 outcomes from every state, and `solve` walks every outcome of every state, so the finish has its own value iteration inside `abstraction`, over outcome tables shared between states, in numpy (a profile found the need: `solve` takes 136 seconds and 1.2 GB for a five-socket fixture of 6.2 million transitions, the routine 0.1 seconds and 38 MB). `solver` itself is unchanged.

The goal is about 1 minute per benchmark query, tracked by the benchmark command and never asserted in tests (ADR-0014).

Two-input actions need a branching (least fixed point) solver; it arrives in B3 (ADR-0011).

Origin: [Design grill round 1: foundations](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/2) Q9 and "Settled before the grill" (Python first); [solver engineering research](../research/2026-10-30-4-solver-engineering.md).

## Amendment: the Peek kind (B8, [Grill B8](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/106), 2026-11-01)

`solver` gains one kind beside `Action`: `Peek(label, cost, options, fallback)`, stdlib, with a non-linear but monotone and continuous backup computed by a sorted-tail sweep (ADR-0054). Value iteration from zero, policy iteration under a fixed acceptance set and the certificate stay as written; the exact self-loop elimination does not apply to a burn that returns to the Lock's own state, which the policy expansion turns into an ordinary cycle; a reaction that never accepts is an improper policy. `solver` still knows nothing of Path of Exile.

Built as B8-1 (#293): the kind is `Peek(label, cost, options, fallback)` in `solver._model`, and `solve_peek` (`solver._peek`) holds the rest behind one seam, with `solve` and `_certify` untouched. `solve_peek` runs policy iteration on the reaction around `solve`: a Lock-free solve (the start, proper), then rounds of "acceptance set from the values by the sorted-tail sweep, expand the Peek into ordinary actions and pseudo-states (`ExpandedModel`), `solve`" until the acceptance sets stop changing. This is the least fixed point of ADR-0022 reached from above by a sequence of affine problems, so the certificate is `solve`'s own on the last expansion. It does not merge states and does not touch the graph walk, so a change to the walk (#312) does not touch it; it asks the model for `actions` once per round, forwards the model's `prefetch` hook (#250) for the model's own states, and gives the same answer for any number of workers. A Peek state whose Lock-free value is infinite (nothing but a Lock leads on from it; the planner's restart makes that impossible there) starts from a reaction that a finite-state analysis of the Peek (`proper_levels`) shows proper. A model with no Peek is solved by one `solve` and the answer is the same bit for bit.

## Amendment: scipy for one preconditioned solve (#1484, 2026-11-23)

`solver` gains scipy, in one file, `solver/_packed_ilu.py`, imported only when a policy's evaluation needs it (`tests/test_solver_independence.py` holds the rule: numpy in the `_packed*.py` files, scipy in that one). The measured case: R17's first plan (H20 of board #1434, the probe of #1467, `.claude/scratch/t1311c/`) is a policy of 1,024,996 states, one strongly connected part of 943,748 with a single exit at 0.0089 per step and 712,952 states on a free action. The plain BiCGSTAB of `_packed_linear.solve` stalls at a backward error of 0.0957, and the exact solve by the cycles of the chain needs 25,133 cut rows, 1.96e9 cells of the Woodbury step against the 6e8 a run allows; a direct sparse LU (SuperLU's `splu`, COLAMD) did not finish in 25 minutes and passed a 16 GB cap. The same system with one unknown per kernel the policy uses (5.2M entries, no kernel expanded state by state) is factored incompletely by SuperLU (`spilu`, drop tolerance 1e-4, fill at most 20 times) in 146 s and 3 GB, and the planner's BiCGSTAB preconditioned by those factors reaches a backward error of 6.6e-16 in 13 steps. An incomplete LU of a general sparse matrix is not a hot loop to write in numpy: it is SuperLU's, so the dependency is scipy's.

The step is tried only when the plain iteration has stalled (or met its rail) and the exact solve by the cycles cannot be made; where the cycles fit they are solved exactly as before, and their answer is kept even when its backward error is above what an exact answer is taken at (their answer is good to the rounding of the sums, the preconditioned one to its forward estimate: about 1e-8 on long sums). Its answer is an iterate's, taken only when its forward estimate is within 1e-6, and the policy's values are then certified as any others (`certify`). The factorisation asks the run's memory net first (`parallel.active_memory_net`: room for each factor at 20 times the matrix, and the matrix's copies); once begun it is one call that no net can cut. A run whose iteration never stalls takes the same path as before, to the bit.
