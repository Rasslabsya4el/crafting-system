# The Exalted Orb plans the `actions:` key hides on J01 and V01 (#637)

Ticket [#637](https://github.com/Rasslabsya4el/crafting-system/issues/637), open point of #475 (PR #614). Checked 2026-11-13 on main `533a898`, game data repoe-fork 3.29.3.3, Craft of Exile copy fetched 2026-11-13 on the runners (`package.js` and `poec_data.json` hashes as in `tests/fixtures/coe_recorded/b1_core.json`, data 3.29.0.3). Rules: OR-1, OR-5, OR-8, OR-12, OR-13, OR-14, OR-21, OR-24, OR-26, OR-29.

## The question

With the Exalted Orb added to the committed key (`.github/scripts/actions_key.py probe`, run 42378616946), J01's plan costs 49% less and V01's 69% less. Is the rule "an Exalted Orb on a rare jewel or helmet adds one mod from the pool with the same weights, under the cap on mods" right, and is the orb's price right? And where does a kind-level relevance test live?

## Verdict

**The mechanics are right; J01's saving stands at today's price, V01's shrinks from 69% to 36% at today's price.** No planner code changes. Open points filed: #848 (the relevance test), #852 (V01's price file), #857 (the naive crafter's reroll count on a jewel).

### 1. One Exalted Orb, the planner against the emulator (OR-21, OR-24)

Seven cases on the states where the plans with the orb added take it (`tools/coe_emulator/checks/637-exalt-key.json`): the V01 plan's states were listed with the envelope solver (1,360 states take the orb: 102 hold three mods, 468 four, 790 five); J01's are the 3-mod jewels that miss one goal mod (a jewel holds 2 prefixes and 2 suffixes). 12,000 emulator uses per case (heavy run 43356004413, 3 Windows runners, seeds 6470 to 6676). The planner's chance that one use ends in the target is worked by hand from the empty base's pool weights (the wanted mod's weight over what the orb can still add, without the families the item holds) and equals the planner's to 1e-12 (`tests/test_coe_recorded_b1_core.py`, `EXALT_BY_HAND`):

| case | item holds | the orb can add | ours, by hand | emulator | z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J01, wants Attack Speed | Life %, Wand speed, a filler suffix | a suffix | 500/17,520 = 2.854% | 364 (3.033%) | +1.18 |
| J01, wants Wand speed | Life %, Attack Speed, a filler suffix | a prefix | 500/17,900 = 2.793% | 329 (2.742%) | -0.34 |
| J01, wants Life % | Wand speed, Attack Speed, a filler suffix | a prefix | 350/17,750 = 1.972% | 238 (1.983%) | +0.09 |
| V01, +1 Minion, 3 mods | +1 Minion, two filler suffixes | either | 1,000/97,400 = 1.027% | 126 (1.050%) | +0.25 |
| V01, +1 Minion, 4 mods (the plan's second orb) | +1 Minion, a filler prefix, two filler suffixes | either | 1,000/90,400 = 1.106% | 120 (1.000%) | -1.11 |
| V01, +1 Minion, 5 mods | +1 Minion, a filler prefix, three suffixes | a prefix | 1,000/32,000 = 3.125% | 368 (3.067%) | -0.37 |
| V01, Life T1, 3 mods | tier 1 Life, two filler suffixes | either | 250/87,675 = 0.285% | 31 (0.258%) | -0.55 |

Every mod, shape and goal-line cell of the seven cases passes the module's exact binomial test (Bonferroni over the module, alpha 0.01), the emulator refused nothing, and the cap holds: every jewel ends 2 prefixes and 2 suffixes, every 5-mod helmet 3 and 3. The emulator's target count is read by the planner's own judge (`tools/coe_emulator/target_judge.py`), which now reads the target's own price file (V01) and the rarity the case was recorded at (a later item of the craft); before, it could count only the start item.

The independent naive crafter (`tests/naive_crafter/`, which plays every recorded case from the game's own rules) failed the three jewel cases: it gave a rare jewel the 3 prefixes and 3 suffixes of other items, so its Exalted Orb added a third prefix to a jewel that held two. That was a gap of the naive crafter, not of the planner: it now has the law `jewel_capacity` (claim `affix_count.rare_jewel_max_per_side`, the wiki's "(1 to 2) prefixes and (1 to 2) suffixes"), and a test turns the law off and sees the jewel case fail. Its jewel reroll count (3 or 4 mods, not 4 to 6) is still the other items'; no case plays it yet (#857).

### 2. The orb's price (OR-8)

The probe priced the Exalted Orb at 2.29 Chaos (poe.ninja, the 2026-10-31 snapshot; V01's own price file of 2026-11-01 holds the same row). Today's snapshot (`tests/fixtures/prices/routes-allflame-2026-11-10.json`) has 3.61 Chaos on a deep line (poe.ninja, about 1,600 units a day; the trade site 4.0 Chaos on 197 units). A deep line: no thin-market question as in #627 and #693.

Re-measured with the envelope search (heavy run 43355975224, one Ubuntu runner, `actions_key.py one`; J01 at the 2026-11-10 prices, V01 at its own price file):

| route | committed key | key plus the orb | change | states walked | seconds | peak MB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J01 | 34,288.31 | 17,468.70 | -49.05% | 8 -> 6,132 | 0.3 -> 10.1 | 252 -> 1,248 |
| V01 (orb at 2.29) | 858.40 | 263.66 | -69.3% | 786 -> 6,226 | 1.5 -> 11.0 | 249 -> 324 |
| V01, the orb at 3.61 (local, envelope, 14 s) | 858.40 | 546.39 | -36.3% | | | |

J01 hardly moves with the orb's price (the plan rerolls with Chaos Orbs; in the probe only 33 of its states took the orb). V01 does: its plan exalts every helmet twice, so at today's price the saving is about half of the probe's. V01's price file is of 2026-11-01 while every other measurement reads the 2026-11-10 snapshot; moving it is a data change of its own, filed as #852.

## Where the kind-level relevance test lives

Before the walk and before the bound's build, where the offers of a target are made (`cli/_offers.py`), not inside the walk and never as a per-target list (OR-5). It must be exact (OR-12, OR-14): a kind that adds no goal mod can still matter (Annulment opens a slot, a metamod blocks, a Harvest swap turns a resistance into the asked one). It is class C2 of the speed classification #812; filed as #848, with J01 (8 states, 17 of 28 kinds that add none) as its smallest test. It will not remove the kinds that make the graphs grow (essences, the Exalted and Fracturing Orbs, fossil sets: they can add a goal mod), so the size of those graphs stays the search's question (#812 C3, C4).

## What this means for #405

As far as #637 goes, #405 may proceed: the plans the keys of J01 and V01 hide are legal crafts the planner models correctly. Once the keys are out, V01's mean rests on its price file, so its re-measure should be at today's prices. #405 still needs #848 (the relevance step its own decision asks for) and the blockers named in its triage comment.
