# A key no better than the reroll: the lemma #1360 was missing, counted on R24, R13 and R04 (research, 2026-11-20)

Ticket #1363. Research, no planner code. It continues `docs/research/2026-11-20-proof-size-root.md` (#1360), whose words it keeps: a **key** is the exact bound's summary of an item; to **work a key out** is to build every offer's row at it; a **tip** is a key not worked out; the **floor** is the cheapest offer's price, today's value of a tip; `V` is the value of a dump's complete model, `V0` its value at the start key; a **certificate** is a set of keys worked out and a value on them that proves "every plan costs at least 99% of `V0`" (#1360, section 1). #1360 counted that with tips at the floor a certificate needs at least 38,368 of R24's keys, and that if a tip were known, without working it out, to be no better than the start (or than the item after an erasing reroll), R24 would need 87 to 94 keys and R13 328 to 380. It read that fact from the truth (`FREE=oracle`, `FREE=mode`); this note looks for a rule that gives it from the key alone.

Rules read: OR-12 (nothing is dropped for being unlikely: the lemma below is proved from the game's rules, and a rule that only holds on the dumps is said to be only that), OR-38 (no proof is claimed from a count: the certificates are counts on three dumps, not a proof of any route), OR-39 (the 1% stop).

**Every figure below is a count that was run** on the owner's server on 2026-11-20, one process and one thread a count, load 6 to 7 beside it. The dumps are #1360's (`.claude/scratch/t1360/e-R24.npz`, `e-R13.npz`, `e-R04.npz` and their bisimulation blocks); the scripts are in `research/proof-size/` (section 8), their outputs in `research/proof-size/out/`.

## Short answer

- **Found: an exact lemma that gives "no better than the reroll" from the key alone, and with it the smallest proof falls to hundreds of keys on R24 and R13.** On R24 a 1% certificate then needs **at least 735 keys, and one of 764 is found** (against at least 38,368 with tips at the floor: 50 times fewer), plus 43 shared reroll outcomes (kernels) built once. On R13 it needs **at least 326 keys, one of 378 is found** (against at least 1,078 read from the merge), plus 21 shared kernels. On R13 that is the oracle's own figure (327.7 / 380, #1360); on R24 it is 8 times the oracle's 87 / 94, because 1,368 of R24's keys are not covered (section 3).
- **The lemma ("only a reroll can"):** take the target's leaves that only a reroll can make pass (an essence-only mod, a mod no offer but a reroll adds, a natural mod where the other offers add only crafted ones). A key on which those leaves do not already satisfy the target can reach the goal only through one of those reroll rows, applied at a key that still fails. A reroll's outcome does not depend on the mods it erases, so its row is one shared kernel per context (rarity, fractured mods). Hence `V(k) >= z(context of k)`, where `z` is the cheapest of those few rows, or of a context change (Scouring, the restart) followed by one. On R24, in its 11-offer model, `z` is exactly `V0`: **every key that lacks Avoid Shock or a tier 1 Spell Suppression is no better than the start.** On R13, `z` is the value of the item after a reroll (28,163.95 c at a rare belt, 28,164.02 c at the bought belt). The proof is in section 2; the game data it rests on (spawn weights 0, the fire tag) was read from the 3.29.3.3 export.
- **It holds on every key of the dumps**: on R24 (59,527 keys of 60,904), R13 (77,722 of 78,654) and R04 (98,819 of 100,773) no row of another offer ever leads out of the set, and no key's dumped value is below its `z` (0 exceptions each). The lower model it gives is exact at the start on R24 and R13 (its start value equals `V0` to the last digit) and **95.4% of `V0` on R04**.
- **On R04 it does not reach 1%** (no certificate exists with it alone: the program is infeasible): R04's start must still fracture the right mod, a gamble the lemma values at its best outcome. At 5% and 10% it gives proofs of 1,694 to 1,825 and 1,597 to 1,823 keys. Applied only to keys that already hold a fractured mod (the unfractured keys worked out), it is exact at the start (99.99996% of `V0`) and gives a 1% proof of **at least 11,470 keys, 12,257 found** (against at least 46,518 read from the merge, and 16,056 / 17,203 with Proposal A, #1367): better than either, still thousands (section 5).
- **Ruled out, with counts:** (1) on R24, "a Veiled Chaos Orb on locked suffixes adds no suffix" is false: 112 of its rows lead out of the set (`out/t1363-lemma-R24-core.log`); the one read by hand, key 448 of the dump, has Avoid Shock, a junk suffix and an open suffix slot under the lock, and the orb adds a T1 Suppress there (the lock keeps the suffixes, it does not stop new ones); the other 111 were not read one by one; the lemma counts every Veiled Chaos Orb row. (2) One value `z` for all contexts is sound but useless on R13: its lower model collapses to 8,882 c at the start, because an Alchemy valued without the Scouring before it is a reroll of 0.12 c instead of 0.47 c, and the difference is paid once per try, about 10,000 tries (section 4). (3) Six rules that try to name the bisimulation blocks from the key alone (question (b)) all mix blocks (section 6). The blocks are not named; with the lemma they need not be.
- **What it means for the work:** the lemma is a **lead for the bound's tips** that is exact where it applies, cheap (a test of the key's statuses and a fixed point over a few shared kernels), and turns R24's and R13's proofs into hundreds of keys. Filed as #1370 (the reroll lemma in the bound). Its limit: the dumps hold 11 and 72 offers; a full catalog with an adder (Exalted Orb, Regal Orb, Augmentation) for a leaf of the lemma makes that adder a raiser at every failing key with an open slot, and its rows are not shared. Whether that keeps the saving is not counted (section 7).

## 1. What the blocks are

The ticket asked first to read the largest bisimulation blocks and say what their members share.

**R24** (`e-R24.npz`: 11 offers with the restart, 60,904 keys, 940 blocks). Its leaves: 0 the fractured Mana, 1 the unveiled Movement Speed, 2 Avoid Shock (the Deafening Essence of Torment's mod), 3 tier 1 Spell Suppression, 4 to 6 the three resistances at tier 2 (at least one). The ten largest blocks have 1,536 keys each, the next twenty 768. One of the largest, in words: rare boots with the fractured Mana, **no Avoid Shock and no Spell Suppression**, two suffixes of which a resistance pair in eight states (leaf 4 below or passing, leaf 5 or 6 below or passing), a veiled suffix slot, and an unveil waiting for its pick with three junk veiled mods on offer (192 combinations of names). Every member is worth `V0` plus the floor (3,617.64 c). What they share is that the item lacks Avoid Shock, which only the essence adds, and the essence rerolls every mod but the fractured one; what differs (the resistances' tiers, the junk on offer) is exactly what that reroll erases.

**R13** (`e-R13.npz`: 72 offers with the restart, 78,654 keys, 1,135 blocks). Leaves: 0 tier 1 Life, 1 to 3 tier 1 Fire, Cold, Lightning resistance (two of three), all not crafted; 4 to 6 the three bench crafts. The largest block has 5,184 keys: rare belts whose natural mods fail (96 status combinations, e.g. Life and Fire below tier 1), with 180 combinations of bench-crafted mods, one or two prefixes and suffixes, with or without a veiled slot. Every member is worth 28,163.95 c, the value of Harvest Reforge Fire, the best reroll. They share that their natural mods cannot pass without a reroll (every other offer of the dump adds only crafted mods or removes); what differs (crafted mods, veiled slots, tiers below 1) is erased by that reroll.

So the blocks' members share **"this item needs a reroll that erases what differs between us"**. That is the candidate below; the block structure itself (why one block and not two) is finer than this and is not named (section 6).

## 2. The lemma

**Statement.** Let `Q` be a set of the target's leaves and `R` the offers that can turn a `Q` leaf from not passing to passing (the **raisers**). Let `F` be the keys (not goals) on which the target is not satisfied when every leaf outside `Q` is counted as passing and the `Q` leaves keep their status. A **context** is what a raiser's outcome may depend on besides its own draw: here the rarity and the fractured mods. Then for every key `k` in `F`:

    V(k) >= z(ctx(k)),   z(X) = min( min over raiser rows a at keys of F in context X of  c_a + sum_s P_a(s) V(s),
                                     min over non-raiser rows b at keys of F that lead to context Y of  c_b + z(Y) )

**Proof.** A row of an offer outside `R` never raises a `Q` leaf, and the leaves outside `Q` already count as passing in the test, so such a row at a key of `F` leads only to keys of `F` (every successor fails the same test). The goal is not in `F`, so every plan from `k` that reaches the goal applies, at some first time, a raiser row at a key `k'` of `F`; before it, it applies only non-raiser rows, each costing at least 0, and those that change the context cost at least the cheapest such move. The plan's expected cost is at least the cost of the moves to `ctx(k')`, plus `c_a + sum P_a(s) V(s)` for the raiser row at `k'`, which is at least `z(ctx(k'))` once the moves are counted; so it is at least `z(ctx(k))`. Nothing is dropped: the bound holds for every plan, whatever its odds (OR-12). The same argument as a sub-solution of the model's equations, which is what the certificate uses (section 3): give every key of `F` the value `z(ctx)`; a raiser row is at least `z` by the definition of `z`, and a non-raiser row costs `c_b >= 0` and leads to keys of `F` in contexts `Y` with `c_b + z(Y) >= z(X)` (a move) or in `X` itself, so its expected value is at least `z(X)` even when the context it lands in is random; so `z` is under the model's value on `F`. **Why it is cheap:** a raiser that rerolls the whole item (an essence on a rare, a Chaos Orb, a Harvest reforge, an Alteration on a magic item) has an outcome that depends only on the context, so its row is one kernel per offer and context, shared by every key; a raiser that keeps part of the item (a Veiled Chaos Orb under the suffix lock keeps the suffixes) has a kernel per kept part (R24: 42 Veiled Chaos Orb kernels and the essence's one, 43 in all); `z` is a fixed point over a handful of numbers. A raiser that adds a mod to the item as it is (an Exalted Orb) has a kernel per key and makes the lemma expensive (section 7).

## 3. The counts

Each candidate is checked on its dump in three ways (`lemma.py`): **(1)** every row of an offer not declared a raiser, at every key of `F`, leads only into `F` (this is the proof's one assumption, checked on every row of the model); **(2)** no key of `F` has a dumped value under the global `z`; **(3)** none under its context's `z`. Then the certificate program of #1360 (`cert.py`) is solved with the lemma (`FREE=` the lemma's file): keys of `F` are not worked out, each is bounded by `W(k) <= z(ctx(k))`, with `z(X) <= c_a + sum P_a W` for every raiser row's kernel and `z(X) <= c_b + z(Y)` for every move. The program's optimum is a lower bound on the keys any certificate with the lemma must work out; the support of its solution (reweighted once) is a certificate found. `vi.py` computes the lower model's own value at the start.

**What the 764 keys of R24 are.** The lemma covers every key that lacks Avoid Shock or a tier 1 Suppress. The 1,368 keys it leaves out have both; 1,245 of them lack a passing resistance and 1,128 hold an unveil's offer; 763 of them are in the certificate found (its 764th key is the start). ## 4. Why one value for all contexts fails (R13)

With one `z` for every key of `F`, R13's lower model is worth 8,882 c at the start, not 28,164 c. The lemma is still true (check (2): no key of `F` is under 28,163.67 c); what fails is the lower model built from it. One `z` lets a rare belt reroll with the Alchemy Orb's row (0.12 c), which needs a normal belt, without paying the Scouring (0.35 c) first. A reroll's chance of a good belt is about 1 in 10,000 here (`9.6e-5` of the Alchemy kernel's mass leaves `F`), so the price of a try is paid about 10,000 times: the 0.35 c skipped becomes thousands of chaos, and the fixed point runs down. With the rarity as the context and Scouring as a move (rare to normal at 0.35 c), the model's start value is `V0` to the last digit and the certificate is 326 / 378. **A lemma of this kind must keep every price a reroll pays per try, exactly; a gap in the price of one try is multiplied by the number of tries.**

## 5. R04

R04's essence leaves (Pierce and Strength) are essence-only, so the lemma holds (0 exceptions on 98,819 keys), but its lower model is worth 95.4% of `V0` at the start: the start has no fractured mod, and the lemma's move "fracture, then reroll" is valued at the Fracturing Orb's price and the **best** fractured context, while the true value pays for the chance of fracturing the wrong mod (#1360: 82,986 of the 100,771 keys cannot reach the goal without the restart). So with the lemma alone no 1% certificate exists on R04; at 5% the proof is 1,694 to 1,825 keys. Applied only to keys with a fractured mod (the 12,829 other keys, those with no fractured mod and those that pass, are worked out, the restart a raiser row, since it leads to the unfractured start), the lemma holds (0 exceptions on 87,942 keys) and its lower model is exact at the start (23,534.08 c against 23,534.09 c, `out/t1363-vi-R04-frac.log`): the gamble of the fracture is then read from the Fracturing Orb's real rows. The smallest 1% certificate is **at least 11,470 keys, 12,257 found** (`out/t1363-c-R04-frac-0.01-ipm.log`, HiGHS interior point, 1,347 s; the simplex gave no result within the 60 minutes it was given, `out/t1363-c-R04-frac-0.01.log` holds its two header lines; the server's load was not logged). That is a quarter of the 46,518 the floor needs and under Proposal A's 16,056; which keys make it up is not counted. Whether Proposal A and this lemma together go lower is not counted. Proposal A (#1367) values the ruined keys exactly and stays R04's lemma; the two are compatible (both are lower bounds, and a key under both takes the larger); their combination is not counted.

## 6. Question (b): naming the blocks before their rows

Six rules read from the key alone were counted as names for the blocks inside `F` (`bnames.py`): a rule is exact on the dump when no class it makes holds keys of two blocks.

**Every rule mixes blocks.** The blocks keep distinctions that matter for rows that are never the best (an Annulment's outcomes differ with the mods it can remove), so a name for them must read what each offer does at the key, which is building its rows. Values are coarser: on R13 the keys of `F` take 4 values and every rule keeps them apart (0 classes mixing values); on R24 9 values, mixed in 12 to 1,290 classes. The lemma does not need the blocks: it bounds all of `F` by its contexts' `z` (2 numbers on R24, 4 on R13), which is coarser than any merge and exact where the proof needs it (at the start). **So (b) is not found, and (a) makes it unnecessary for the proof**; it would still shrink the complete build, which is not the road the lemma opens.

## 7. What this means for the work

**(a) A lead for the bound.** In the build, a tip in `F` is worth `z(ctx)` instead of the floor. `z` comes from the raisers' shared kernels, which the build makes once per context, and a fixed point over the contexts. The test of `F` reads the key's statuses only; the raisers and the contexts are a property of the offers (which leaves an offer can raise, which rarity it can move to), read from the mods' spawn weights and tags and the offers' kinds, not from rows. It is a lower bound at every tip (section 2), so the build stays sound. Counted on the dumps, it makes a 1% proof **764 keys plus 43 kernels on R24 and 378 keys plus 21 kernels on R13**, where the floor needs at least 38,368 and 1,078. Filed as #1370, the first PR to put it into the bound (a prediction: the counts above are of the smallest certificate, not of a build's order; a build led by `z` still has to find those keys).

**(b) The limit, not counted.** The dumps hold 11 offers (R24) and 72 (R13); the default methods offer 4,105 on R24 and 172 to 190 in R13's later bounds (#1325, #1332, #1338). An offer that adds a mod to the item as it is, and can add a `Q` leaf (an Exalted Orb with an open suffix for Spell Suppression on R24; a Regal Orb or an Exalted Orb for a natural Life or resistance on R13), is a raiser at every key of `F` where it applies, and its kernel depends on the key. The lemma stays exact, but `z` then needs those rows at those keys, which is the work it saves. Avoid Shock on R24 and the essence mods on R04 have no such adder in any catalog (spawn weight 0), so the R24 "Avoid Shock only" figure (5,966 / 6,126, 1 kernel) does not depend on adders; it still depends on the essence's row staying one kernel, which a metamod that keeps prefixes (not in the dump's offers) would break, since the essence then keeps them too (an expectation from the game's rules, not counted). How many adders R24's 4,105 and R13's 190 offers hold, and what the certificate is then, is not counted: there is no complete dump of those models (#1368).

**(c) R04** keeps Proposal A (#1367) as its lemma, since it values the ruined keys exactly. This one, applied to all keys, gives no 1% certificate there; applied to the fractured keys alone it gives one of at least 11,470 keys, 12,257 found (section 5), under Proposal A's 16,056; the two together are not counted.

**(d) What stays as #1360 said:** the complete build (#1340 and the line of #1335 to #1358) is still the only known road on R04, and on R24 and R13 until #1370 is measured on the full catalogs.

## 8. The tools

`research/proof-size/` (new here): `lemma.py` (a route's `Q`, raisers and contexts; checks (1) to (3); writes the lemma's file for `cert.py`), `vi.py` (the lower model's value), `bnames.py` (question (b)), `rest.py` (R24's keys outside `F`), `lemma.sh` (every run of this note, in order). `cert.py` gains `FREE=<lemma file>` (one `z` per context, `ONEZ=1` for the single `z` of section 4) and `MAXSTART=1` (the lower model's start value).
