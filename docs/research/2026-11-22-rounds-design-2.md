# The set of methods a search holds, grown from the plan in hand: the second design for the rounds (research, 2026-11-22)

Ticket #1276, PR 1b (this note; no planner code). It replaces the recommendation of `2026-11-18-rounds-design.md` (scheme S, built as draft PR #1404 and refuted by measurement). Question: by what general rule does the set of methods one search holds grow, so that for any target the kinds a good plan needs are together early, without waiting for a complete bound build per step and without enumerating subsets blindly; and where does the 1% proof (OR-39) then come from?

Rules read: OR-12, OR-13, OR-37, OR-38, OR-39, OR-40, OR-41, OR-55, OR-56, OR-58. Inputs: `2026-11-18-rounds-design.md`; draft PR #1404 (branch `claude/t1276b`, head a174afcc) and its two measurements (`.claude/scratch/tmeasS/table.md`, scheme S at 6 h; `.claude/scratch/tmeasure/table.md`, main at 6 h); `2026-11-20-proof-size-root.md`, `2026-11-20-key-dominance-lemma.md`; the retro's groups (`.claude/scratch/retro-1118/groups-*`); issues #1389, #1394, #1395, #1384, #1411, #1158, #1370.

**Every figure below is a run** on the owner's server on 2026-11-22, main 2028e28b (the targets are the guides' finished items since #1289, OR-58), as the site plans (`benchmark_routes.py <route> --net`: the target alone, every beta method, prices `routes-allflame-2026-07-31.json`), 4 worker processes, planner memory net 12 GB, each run in a 16 GB scope. The set a search holds was set from outside by the probe `research/rounds-design/tb.py` (no planner file changed): `LEAD=names:...` puts the named offers in round 1 and main's rounds follow; `ONLY=...` with `LEAD=one` keeps only the named offers, one search. `SPB=0.33` lets the bound's build before a first plan take three quarters of the net instead of a quarter. "1 h" runs set the planner's time net to 3,600 s through `CRAFTING_CONFIG`. The 1-minute load was 10 to 20 through all runs (another ticket agent's heavy runs shared the machine); each row is one run, not repeated. Logs stay on the server (`.claude/scratch/t1276c/<tag>.log`); their key lines (each run's command, models, bounds, stops, plans and dearest steps) are copied in `research/rounds-design-2/runs.txt`, every set in full in `research/rounds-design-2/sets.txt`, and the probe with its scripts in `research/rounds-design-2/` (`tp.py`, `run1.sh`, `queue.sh`).

Words as in the notes before: an **offer** is one priced action; a **kind** a family of offers; the **bound** the planner's lower figure, whose **build** is **complete** when every key it found is worked out. New here: the **support** of a plan is the set of offers its policy uses; a **step** of a plan is one action of its route page, and its **cost share** is its expected uses times its price (the page's "used N times on average, X Chaos in all").

## Short answer

1. **The rule: grow the set from the plan in hand (plan-led growth).** Each search holds the support of the plan in hand plus the few offers that the plan's dearest step names, by the role they would play against that step: an adder for each mod the step waits for, a protector for each mod the step can destroy, a cleaner and a narrower for each adder. Before any plan the set is the seed of PR #1404 (the two offers of least cost over chance per wanted mod); a seed with no plan names its roles the same way, from the mods no offer of it can hold together. A round's set is the old plan's support plus the named offers, never the union of everything tried so far, so it stays near the size of a guide's set (10 to 18 offers), the size whose bound builds. It is a crafter's loop: look at what the route in hand spends its money on, and bring the tool that fixes that.
2. **Measured, the search is fast on the right small set, and every mechanical larger set fails.** With the guide's methods in round 1: R04 35,668.20 c in 26 s (guide's completed route 96,329.85 c), R24 3,658.60 c (bound complete in 141 s, proved within 0.8%), R13 21,846.53 c in 1 s; R17's and R20's bounds do not complete on the guide's own set (R17: 62,934 of 495,932 keys after 30 minutes). The seed plus every tool family together (22 to 35 offers, "tool closure") gives no sane plan on any of R04, R17, R13, R24 in 600 s, nor with three quarters of the net for the build: the build found 352,993 keys on R04 and 71,517 on R17 and was still not complete at 465 s and 463 s; with a 1 h net it was still in its first model after 28 minutes (R04: 2,610,869 keys found, 234 worked out).
3. **Plan-led growth reached the best known plan on R04 in two steps and beat main and the guide on R13 in one.** R04 (main: no plan): seed, no plan (proved in 16 s); step 1 (protectors of the two essence mods) 5,546,685.86 c on a 1 h net, proved within 1% of its 14 offers; step 2 (adders, cleaner, narrowers for the Deafening Essence spam it showed) **35,664.99 c in 303 s, proved within 1% of its 10 offers**, the same plan the guide's set gives. R13: seed, no plan (0 s); step 1, 18 offers, **10,742.39 c**, not proved (its plan came from a second search whose bound stopped, section 6) (main at 600 s 28,234.59 c; guide 21,863.36 c; main at 6 h 6,764.71 c); step 2 (the imprint the Alteration spam names, with Annulment and the Harvest add/remove crafts) **3,757.17 c** in 617 s, cheaper than every plan of R13 known, but not proved: its bound did not complete. R24: seed, step 1 and step 2 each ended with "no plan"; step 2 lacked Pick 3, my error in applying the rule (section 6), and the rule as section 4 first stated it named no veiled chain at all. R24 is not shown. R17 and R20 were not reached: their dearest steps name sets near their guides' sets, whose bounds do not complete (above).
4. **The proof comes in two separate places, and only one exists today.** Each round of plan-led growth is one envelope search with its own bound; when that bound completes, its plan is proved within 1% among that round's offers, and the answer says so with the round's methods listed. The proof over every method is a separate, later phase over the final set (main's widening rounds with the final support as base, and the one closing round of every offer), and on the heavy routes it does not close today: no build of thousands of offers completes (#1158, `2026-11-20-proof-size-root.md`). So a run claims "the cheapest of these methods, within 1%", lists every method it did not hold together with the plan's, and claims nothing over every method until the closing round closes (OR-13, OR-39).
5. **What it costs and what can refute it.** A step costs one search of 10 to 18 offers: seconds to 5 minutes here when the bound completes. Steps are few (2 on R04, 1 on R13 to a plan below the guide), but each must wait for its bound to complete, or the named offers are searched blind, as in every failure above. The rule fails where the plan in hand does not show the missing kind (a plan with no dear step that a named role fixes), where the support plus the named offers are too many for the build (R13's step 2 with the imprint: 28,193 of 51,265 keys at 150 s), and on R17 and R20, whose guide-size sets do not build. Only R04 and R13 were carried to a plan, by hand, one run each, and the hand knew the guides' sets (section 10).

## 1. What scheme S showed, and what stands of it

Draft PR #1404 built scheme S of the first note: a seed, then up to 12 fixed families as nested steps, each step holding every family accepted before it, accepted only when its bound's build completed. Measured at 600 s (`.claude/scratch/handoff-t1276b.md`) and at 6 h (`.claude/scratch/tmeasS/table.md`):

- The builds did not complete once a step held the accepted families: R13's currency-orb step built 1.9 million keys in 1,661 s without completing, so the basic orbs never joined and every later step had "no plan with them alone"; R17's imprint step worked out 1,745 of 87,539 cases in 1,433 s. Of R17's 41 steps, 37 were undone with no search. The time of every undone step was the build's, never the search's.
- So S was dearer than main on 600 s (R13, R08, R09) and on 6 h (R13 12,086,611 c against 6,764.71; R08, R09, R11 killed at 16 GB, #1411), and held no guide's combination on R17 (43.2 million c).
- What stands: the seed alone brought R11 from 4,964.92 to 526.96 c and R10 from 150 s to 0.4 s; with the guide's methods together one search is fast (section 2).

The premise that failed is S's step: "everything accepted so far plus one family". Its sets grow with the run, so its builds grow too. Main's rounds fail the other way (base plus one group, `_rounds.py:332`): small sets that never hold a guide's combination. The rule below keeps the sets small and lets the plan choose what they hold.

## 4. The rule

Through the planner's one entry and the one envelope search; nothing under `cli/`.

**State.** The plan in hand `P` (none at the start), its support `U(P)`, and a record of which steps of `P` were diagnosed.

**Round 0, the seed.** The seed of PR #1404 (`_offer_kinds.leaf_providers`: per wanted mod not passing at the start, the 2 offers of least cost over chance, read at each shape the offers turn the start into). Searched alone.

**A plan in hand: diagnose its dearest step.** Take the step `D` of `P` with the largest cost share not diagnosed yet. At the states where `D` acts, read from the abstraction's statuses: `W`, the wanted mods not passing that `D` can make pass (the mods `D` waits for); `K`, the wanted mods passing that `D`'s outcome can lose (empty when `D` adds or finishes; every passing non-fractured, non-locked mod when `D` rerolls). Name, from the offers' declarations alone (`_offer_kinds._kind_of`, `Adds`, `Abstraction.features`; no kind is named in the code):

- **Adders** for each mod of `W`: the offers of kind ADD or FINISHING with a chance on it at `D`'s shape, the 2 of least cost over chance (on R04 the Exalted Orb; on R13 the Augmentation, Regal and Exalted Orbs; on R24 the Exalted Orb and the Harvest add/remove of the resistances' tags).
- **The moves** an adder needs to apply at `D`'s shape (`reached_by`: Regal from magic to rare, Scouring, Alteration as magic's reroll).
- **Protectors** for each mod of `K`: the offers whose declared outcome keeps that mod under `D`'s kind of reroll: the metamod that locks its side (when `W` sits on the other side), the fracturing offers (when the item has no fractured mod), the imprint with its restore (when `D` acts on an item the imprint takes).
- **Cleaners and narrowers** for each adder named: the cheapest offer that removes a random mod (Annulment), Remove Crafted Mods when the set holds a crafted mod, the metamods that take a kind of mod out of the adder's pool, and the craft that allows more crafted mods when two crafts are held together.

**Dependencies.** Every offer in a round brings what it needs to act, read from `reached_by` and the offers' declarations: a pick brings the Unveil, a veiled-mod source and every other pick; a restore brings its imprint. (Added after R24, section 6; not tested.)

**No plan in hand (the seed or a step proved "no plan"):** the same roles, read at the start for every wanted mod whose providers in the set all reroll (it waits) and every wanted mod that another mod's only provider destroys (it is at risk). R04's two essence mods are each given only by their essence, which rerolls the other: protectors (the fracturing offer, both side locks, Remove Crafted Mods). R13's resistances and life come from erasing essences on a magic belt: moves and adders.

**The next round** holds `U(P)` (the seed when there is no plan), the named offers, and the restart; nothing else of earlier rounds. It is searched with `P`'s certified upper figure as its price cut (`_price_cut`, as today), and its build may use the round's whole share (the quarter of `SEARCH_PER_BUILD` goes for these rounds: on R24's guide set it is what separates a plan below the guide from none, `2026-11-18-rounds-design.md`, section 2). If the round's plan is cheaper, it becomes `P` and the diagnosis starts again at its dearest step. If not, `D` is marked diagnosed and the next dearest step of `P` is diagnosed. A role that names nothing new for any step ends the growth.

**Then the widening.** With the time left, main's rounds run over the remaining groups with `U(P)` as their base (each beaten group set aside, one closing round of every offer when every round closed, `_rounds.widen` as today): the bulk kinds (other essences, bench crafts, Harvest crafts, beasts, fossil sets) are alternative providers a crafter compares, and this is where the answer's list of what was not ruled out comes from (section 7).

**What it guarantees.** (1) The plan in hand never gets dearer (OR-39: a plan is shown as soon as one exists and the shown one is replaced only by a notably better one). (2) Every round holds the plan in hand's support, so a combination the plan already uses is never split (main's fault, #1389). (3) Two tools that pay only together (the G/H example of `_rounds.py`) are named in the same round when one step of the plan waits for both mods, since the adders of every mod of `W` are named at once. (4) Each round is one search with its own bound: when that bound completes, the round's plan is the cheapest of its offers within the gap. (5) A round's size is the support (3 to 8 offers in every plan above) plus the named roles (4 to 10), whatever the catalog's size. (6) Nothing is cut by a count (OR-12): the 2 of the seed and of each role are an order of what is searched first, and every offer still has its group in the widening. When the net ends before the widening reaches a group, the answer names that group as not tried, with its floor, and says the plan is the cheapest only of the methods it lists (OR-13): on the heavy routes that is the normal end today.

**What it does not guarantee.** That the final plan is the cheapest of every method: a cheaper plan of other kinds is not excluded until the widening's closing round closes. That the roles name the right offer: a role is read from declarations, so a tool whose worth shows only through a chain the dearest step does not reveal (an Awakener's Orb's donor) is reached only by the widening. That the rounds build: the named set may still be too large (R13's step 2).

## 7. What the answer claims at each point (OR-13, OR-38, OR-39)

- **During growth, after a round whose bound completed:** "This plan costs X Chaos on average. Among the methods it was searched with (list), no plan costs less than L (within g%)." The list is that round's offers; it is not "every method".
- **After a round whose bound did not complete:** the plan is shown only if it is cheaper than the plan in hand, marked unproved ("its cost is exact; nothing is proved about cheaper plans, even of its own methods"), as `rounds_limits` says today for a stopped round.
- **During the widening:** each group searched with the support and beaten is listed with its figure ("no plan of these methods with the plan's own costs less than Y"); a group not reached is listed as not tried. The G/H caveat of `_rounds.py` stays: a beaten group is not proved useless in combination with a later one.
- **"The cheapest of every method, within 1%"** is said only when the closing round of every offer closes. On the heavy routes no such round closes today: a build of every offer does not complete on R04, R14, R17, R20, R24 (#1158; `2026-11-20-proof-size-root.md`: any 1% proof on today's bound works out most keys of the complete model; R04's round of 1,245 offers stops on the memory net, #1395). Where the proof over every method comes from is therefore not the rounds but the bound: the reroll lemma (#1370) and the ruined-keys state (#1367, landed: "8,495 ruined keys held by one" in R04's runs here) make proofs of hundreds to thousands of keys on the guide-size models of R24, R13 and R04; whether they make the full catalog's model provable is not counted (#1368).

OR-39 is kept: a plan is shown at the first round that has one; it is replaced only by a notably better one, with the threshold set from measurements (OR-39; not measured yet, #914), while the rounds keep every cheaper plan internally; the proof goes on while the run is under its aim and, past it, stops at the first round proved within 1% (OR-39's reading of #925), which for plan-led growth is the first round whose bound completes within 1%; and every plan shown carries the gap actually proved and the methods it is proved against. No mode without a proof is proposed: each round's own proof is a proof, stated with its scope.

## 8. Cost, stops, and the known walls

- **Time.** A round costs its build plus its search. Measured: 1 to 45 s on the rounds whose bound completed quickly (R04 steps 0 and 2, R13 steps 0 and 1 to 109 s, R17 and R20 seeds), 141 to 244 s on R24's guide set and R04's step 1. R04 from the seed to 35,664.99 c: 16 + 464 + 303 s, about 13 minutes on a 1 h net; at the 600 s net with today's quarter, step 1 did not build. The rule needs the round's build to have its whole share (section 4), and the site's net to allow several such rounds; long runs are acceptable (owner, 2026-11-18), a day for an average craft is not, and R04 needed about 13 minutes of runs to its best plan and R13 about 21 (627 + 617 s), not counting the time I spent diagnosing by hand.
- **Memory.** R04's guide-set round peaked at 318 MB (its benchmark record); the other runs' peaks were not recorded. The 16 GB kills of #1411 came from S's big steps and main's long walks after a stopped bound; a small round with a complete bound walks thousands of states, not millions. The widening phase keeps main's memory walls (#1395: R04's growth doubling to the 12 GB net in its fossil round; #1411).
- **What stops it.** The net (the plan in hand stands, and the answer lists what was not held); a round whose build does not complete in its share (its offers are searched blind and its plan kept only if cheaper, then the next step is diagnosed); a diagnosis that names nothing new.
- **#1394** (R04's first plan's walk cut by time after a stopped bound) does not arise in a round whose bound completes: the first plan is the bound's own. **#1384** (the workers rarely run): the rule changes nothing there; the cores busy were not measured in these runs. **#1158** is the wall for R17 and R20 and for every round the rule names too large (R13's step 2), and it stays the critical path.

## 10. What the experiments did not show, and what could refute the design

- The diagnosis was applied **by hand**, from the plan's text report, on three routes; no code makes it. **The hand knew the guides' sets**: R04's step 2 is the guide's labels plus two offers, and the R17, R20, R14 "by reading" rows of section 5 name the guide's kinds. A coded diagnosis that never saw a guide is the test this note could not run. A coded diagnosis reading statuses and declarations may name more or other offers than I did (I named both narrowing metamods and the multimod on R04 where the guide uses one metamod; I named Harvest add/remove of all three resistance tags on R24 and R13).
- **One run per row**, under a load of 10 to 20; the 1 h rows' times are not repeated.
- The rule reached a plan at or below the guide on **R04 and R13 only** (R13's best, 3,757.17 c, unproved). R24's step 2 is in section 6; R17, R20, R14, R06 were not carried past their seeds, and on R17 and R20 the evidence (their guide sets do not build in 2 h and 600 s) says it would not help until #1158 is fixed.
- R04 needed the build allowed past the quarter. With a 600 s net (a machine setting, OR-40; the owner dropped the ten-minute planning net on 2026-11-18) and today's quarter, R04's step 1 does not build and the rule stalls there, as S did.
- R24 was not shown: every set tried ended with no plan, the last because I left Pick 3 out. The rule needs a dependency role (an offer brings what it needs to act: a pick brings the Unveil, a veiled-mod source and every pick) that section 4 now states but no run tested.
- **What could refute it:** a target whose dearest step's fix is a reroll (R12's Harvest reforge, a fossil set) and whose widening then holds too much to build; a target where the plan in hand is cheap enough that the dearest step is not the one to fix (the rule then diagnoses steps in order, at one round each, and may run out of net); targets where the support plus the named roles exceed the size that builds (R13's step 2 already does, at 15 offers with the imprint). And the claim that rounds of 10 to 18 offers build at all rests on R04, R13 and R24; R17's and R20's show it does not hold everywhere.

- The proof over every method is not reached by this design. It is not measured here and depends on #1158, #1370 and #1368.
